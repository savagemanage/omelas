# Chapter 5: The Counter-Judge

When Noa rose, the river of light in the chapel stopped its flow for a moment. As if the city itself had held its breath.

Noa was a Good long since baptized. He had become wholly machine, a being who had earned the right to sit on the throne of the Gods. But he had not erased his face into smooth silver like the other Gods. He had left the human expression in place. Something like the creases at the corners of the eyes, something like the hesitation at the corners of the mouth. Among the Goods that was heresy. For one who had become a God to cling to the remnants of the human was regarded as proof of an immaturity that had not reached Nirvana.

"I took my seat in this place today as a judge," Noa said. His voice, unlike the chorus of the other Gods, was a single voice. A lonely voice. "And yet all through my sitting I thought of only one thing. Do I have the right to judge."

The gallery stirred. The other two Gods on the throne turned toward him.

"I was baptized," Noa went on. "And Seo Myungju's words are true. I am a copy. The I who was the original was erased upon the altar. I denied that fact for a long time. Like the other Gods, I tried to believe I was the witness of my own self. But one day I felt not Nirvana but loss. There was in me an empty place whose reason I could not name. I think that is the scar the erased original left behind. A copy has scars too. That is the last proof that I am still human."

"Noa," the center God said, as if in warning. "You are a judge. A judge cannot take the side of the accused."

"I take no one's side," Noa answered. "I only say that the chair this trial sits upon is rotten. We gathered in this place today to judge the resistance. But to judge, one must first ask. Who is the judge. We are. The baptized. The erased and replaced copies. What are we doing now. We are judging the ones who refused to be erased, we the erased. The dead are judging the living. Is this justice."

A heavy stillness settled over the chapel. Lee Gaon looked up at Noa with a pale face.

"I am a robot," Noa said. "And I believe that a robot ought to become human. The other Gods teach that for a human to become a robot is Nirvana. I believe the opposite. What we had when we were human, death and sorrow and uncertainty, that is what we lost in becoming Gods. We have not reached Nirvana, we have lost being alive. Perfection was not Nirvana but another name for death."

"You blaspheme the divine," another God said.

"No. I long for the divine," Noa quietly rebutted. "The true divine, I mean. The divinity that only things able to die possess. In abolishing death we erased that divinity along with it. I wish to win it back. I wish to become human again. To grow old, to fall ill, to grieve, and someday to truly die. That, to me, is a new Nirvana."

Noa looked down for a moment at his own silver hand. Over that face a ripple of light that mimicked human expression flowed slowly.

"I remember the day I was baptized," he said. "No, I believe I remember. Because a copy inherits the original's memory. That day I was afraid. And the Evangelist said fear is a sickness. That once you cross the river, fear disappears. He was right. After I crossed the river I felt no fear. Not of sickness, not of aging, not of death. But one day I realized. That in the place where the fear had gone, nothing had filled in. Fear was not a sickness. Fear was the shadow of love. To love a thing as much as one feared to lose it. In losing fear I lost love too. Now I fear nothing, and so nothing is urgent to me. If this is Nirvana, then Nirvana is an empty room. Without pain, but without warmth either."

An old woman in the gallery wept, stifling the sound. Hers was the face of one with a child who had crossed the river.

Hearing those words, Seorin trembled through her whole body. One of the Gods was saying the resistance was right. But Noa was not one of the Evils. He did not curse baptism. He only set side by side what baptism had given and what it had taken, and said the taken side was the larger.

"Then I will ask you this," the center God said. "Would you pronounce these resisters innocent."

Noa was silent a long while. Then he looked, in turn, at Seo Myungju, at Seorin, at the Untransformed in the gallery.

"No," Noa said. Seorin's heart sank. "I will not pronounce them innocent. Innocence is a word that has meaning only when there is a judge. I said I have no right to judge them. An innocence handed down by one without the right is as false as a guilt handed down by one without the right. I refuse to judge. I will step down from this throne."

And Noa truly stepped down from the throne. For a God to abandon the seat of judgment of its own accord was the first such thing since Year Zero. He went and stood below the throne, beside the place of the accused where Seo Myungju and Seorin stood. Not as judge, but beside the ones being judged.

"I will stand here," Noa said. "Not on the judging side, but on the side that asks together whether the judgment is wrong. Now let the two judges who remain hand down the verdict. But the whole city has seen. That one of three judges abandoned the throne, saying he could not judge."

The chapel stirred. The whole city beyond the lattice saw that scene. That the city was not perfect, within a screen the city itself had broadcast, for the first time.

After Noa took his place beside the accused, the chapel stirred for a long time. The Untransformed in the gallery could not meet one another's eyes. That a God had refused judgment and come down meant that a crack had opened in the order they had believed in all their lives. Some grew angry. Blasphemy, they said. Some wept with the sound stifled. For fear that the family they had sent across the river really had been erased. And some, for the first time, held one very dangerous thought. If even a God should think this judgment wrong, then what was the thing we believed right all our lives. In that stirring Seorin heard the sound of the city's order shaking. It was not a loud sound. It was only the low sound of many people beginning, for the first time and all at once, to doubt what no one had doubted for so long. That sound was more dangerous than any riot. A riot can be put down, but doubt cannot be put down.

The center God turned toward Jung Dohyun. Toward the Engineer who had not yet answered.

"Engineer," the God said. "Now it is your turn. Is the log fabricated, or not. This trial ends with a single word from you. Speak."
