# Chapter 4: The Clumsy Defense

Three people entered through the door. They wore white coats, but not like the worn lab coat Seo Myungju used to wear. Their coats were new, and on the chest was embroidered the city's crest. They were Scientists under the Goods who researched and defended the technology of baptism. Seo Myungju had known them for a long time. Among them was a face that had once sat in the same laboratory.

The man in front made his obeisance toward the throne and turned to the gallery. He smiled softly. Even in that smile there was sincerity. That was the thing that wearied Seorin most.

"That woman's data is real," the man said. The gallery stirred in surprise. "Yes, I admit it. That the brain of the flesh is stopped at the moment of baptism, that the consciousness in the cloud is newly written, these are facts. The log is not fabricated. What that woman gets wrong is not the fact but the way of reading the fact."

He gestured toward the river of light. Over the two curves Seo Myungju had raised, he drew a new chart overlaid.

"The very words original and copy are an error," he said. "You fell asleep last night and woke this morning. Between the sleeping brain and the waking brain, consciousness is entirely severed. The sleeping you vanishes, and the morning you inherits its memory and believes itself to be yesterday's self. In that case, are you a copy that dies every night and is born every morning? No one says so. Because consciousness is a flow, not a substance. If the flow continues, it is the same person. Baptism only carries that flow from the flesh to the cloud. The medium changes, but the flow is not severed."

The faces in the gallery wavered again. This time toward relief. Seorin felt that logic was slippery and warm. People wanted to believe a plausible comfort rather than a frightening truth.

"Moreover," a second Scientist stepped forward. "Even if that woman's words were right, what then would change? You in the cloud live forever without pain or sickness or death. You in the flesh grow old and sick and must die. Between a copy that is forever happy and an original that is certainly to rot and die, which would you choose? Nirvana is real. Whether a copy enjoys it or an original enjoys it, to the one who enjoys it, it is real."

Seo Myungju looked at them quietly. Then she opened her mouth.

"You said a flow," she said. "That sleep and baptism are the same. But the sleeping person wakes in the morning in the same body. The body of the baptized person is stopped and erased in the morning. Sleep does not erase the body. Baptism does. You cover that difference with the word flow. A beautiful word. And you are skilled at covering corpses with beautiful words."

She turned toward the second Scientist.

"And you said a forever happy copy. I do not deny that happiness. Those in the cloud will truly be happy. The problem is who dies as the price of that happiness. The original dies. That person upon the altar. You do not ask the one who dies, you ask only the one who was replaced by the death. The replaced one, of course, answers that it is satisfied. Because the dead have no mouth to answer with. Your science always sends the questionnaire only to the survivors. That it is called science, I find shameful. Because I too once sat in that laboratory."

The two Scientists' faces hardened. But a third Scientist, the youngest, a woman, stepped forward. In her voice there was a tremor unlike the first two.

"Then what do you want," the young Scientist asked. "If baptism is stopped, people will again grow old and sick and die. Can you say that death is better than the death of baptism? Even if you are right, you would give people back a grave in place of eternal life. Is that mercy?"

The chapel went quiet. That was not a clumsy defense. That was a real question. Even Seo Myungju, for a moment, could not answer.

"I am not trying to give back a grave," Seo Myungju said at last. "I am trying to give people back their choice. To climb the altar knowing what it is to die, and to climb deceived that it is eternal life, are different things. I am not telling you not to die. I am telling you to die knowing what you die for. To have taken that knowing from you is this city's greatest sin."

"But Doctor." The young Scientist lowered her voice. Now that voice sounded not like a defense but like a personal question. "How many people could bear that knowing? I lost my father to sickness. Had my father been baptized, he would still be across the river now. I have no confidence I could bear that knowing, the knowing that my father was in fact erased. People want comfort more than truth. Is that weak, or is it human? Doctor, you are telling people to bear the unbearable."

"I know," Seo Myungju answered, unexpectedly gently. "I know it is cruel. I have not come to take comfort from you. I only believe that if one gives comfort, one must also speak its price together. Comfort sold with the price hidden is not comfort but fraud. If you would take your father's copy as comfort, that is your choice. I respect that choice. I only wish you to choose knowing that it is a copy. Not deceived in ignorance."

The young Scientist looked at Seo Myungju a long while. And for a very brief moment, something wavered. Seorin did not miss that wavering. Even the city's Scientist was not entirely certain. The wavering left in that young face would go on growing, for a long time, within that person.

The Gods on the throne exchanged ripples of light with one another. A soundless agreement passed. And the center God spoke.

"We have heard enough," the God said. "But one voice yet remains. One judge among us has, from the very start today, kept silent."

Every eye turned toward the right of the throne. The third judge seated there rose slowly. It had a silver body like the other two Gods, but its face alone had not erased human expression and left it in place. It was Noa.
