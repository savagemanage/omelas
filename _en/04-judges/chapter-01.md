# Chapter 1: The Ones Who Returned

Outside there was no road, but the city had patience.

Two seasons had passed since they left. The five moved among the ruins the lattice could not reach, planting seeds. To the Untransformed scattered along the old roadsides, to the old people and the children the city's evangelism had not yet wholly swallowed, Seo Myungju quietly carried the story that the one smiling across the river was not your brother. Most did not believe. A few did. A few is enough, Seo Myungju said.

But the city was counting its lost sheep. Even into the ruins beyond the lattice the city's eye reached slowly. A small relay climbing an abandoned transmission tower, a single seed of surveillance planted along an old road. One dawn, the white-robed attendants surrounded a shuttered school building. There were no guns, no chains. They only stood before the door, holding lamps, with tender voices.

"Come home," the attendant said. "No one will punish you. The city does not hate you. It only worries. Do not freeze to death in this cold place. Come back to a warm house."

Seorin knew that voice. It was the voice of the day her parents went to be baptized, the voice of the current that had swallowed Taesik. The voice that erases through love.

They scattered and ran. Jun bolted through the back door with Mir in his arms, and Seo Myungju, the seed bag on her back, pulled Seorin along. But the ruins were not the city, and it was easy to lose one's direction in the dark. As dawn broke, the attendants caught Seorin and Seo Myungju. Jun and Mir, and one other, vanished into the dark. What could be given up to capture and what had to be kept alive, Seo Myungju divided in the last moment. The seed bag was on Jun's back.

"The bag?" Seorin asked, held fast.

"Jun has it," Seo Myungju answered low. "That is enough."

The attendants did not handle the two roughly. They wrapped them in blankets, offered warm tea, and put them in a smooth car returning to the city. Beyond the window the ruins fell away, and before long the lattice covered the sky again. Light like an aurora flowed over Seorin's face. It was the city's sky seen again after two seasons. Still beautiful. Still a lie.

Seorin thought back on the two seasons of life outside. The nights in the ruins were cold, food was always short, and she never slept deeply, not knowing when the white robes might appear. In the city she had never once gone hungry. She had never shivered in the cold. And yet in that full, warm city she had always felt empty, while in these cold and hungry ruins she felt, for the first time in a long while, that she was alive. The night Seo Myungju showed Jun the stars, the day they shared the seeds Taesik had left with the old Untransformed, the evening Jun laughed for the first time while speaking of his older brother. Those moments were brighter than any perfect night in the city. Seorin thought it strange, that hardship makes a person more alive. Perhaps what the city had abolished was not only pain but the warmth that comes of enduring pain together. Comfortable people had no need of one another. Only the hungry shared bread.

"What will they do with us," Seorin asked.

"They will not kill us," Seo Myungju said, looking out the window. "The city hates the word kill. They will put us on trial. In a public place. To make us be baptized. To make us say, with our own mouths, that we were wrong. There is no better evangelism than the sight of a resister kneeling of her own accord."

In the car Seorin watched the Gangnam streets slide past the window. Two seasons ago, it was on this very street that she had first been stopped in her tracks by Lee Gaon's sermon. People still came and went with bright faces. The baptized laughed without care, and the Untransformed lived waiting for the day they would someday cross. Nothing seemed to have changed. Whether Seorin had fled or been caught, whether Taesik had died or lived, the city shone on without the slightest tremor. Suddenly Seorin remembered the thing the child David was said to have felt before the river his brother had drowned in. That so great a thing had happened, and the river had not changed in the least. The city was the same. Even when a person was erased, even when a resister was caught, the city lay still, leaving not a single ripple. That stillness angered Seorin most of all. But Seorin knew now. That even beneath a still surface, the water flows. Beneath the city that was quiet on its face, Seo Myungju's seeds, Jung Dohyun's wavering, Jun's stars, were quietly flowing.

The car entered the heart of the city. On the old boulevard of Gangnam, on the spot where the first chapel of Year Zero had stood, there now rose a vast white hall. It was called the Hall of Judgment. But the name fixed to it was not judgment. The letters carved above the door read thus: **the House of Reconciliation**.

Seorin looked at those letters a long while. The city did not punish. The city reconciled. Except that this reconciliation meant that the erased party closed its eyes in gratitude to the party that erased it.

When they stepped out of the car, white-robed people lined both sides of the road and sang. It was a song of a lost sheep come home. On their faces was a joy that was genuine. Seorin confirmed again the fact the two seasons outside had taught her, that this joy was more terrible than hatred. One who hates can be persuaded. One who loves cannot be persuaded.

The doors of the hall opened. Cool and fragrant air flowed out from within. Seo Myungju gripped Seorin's hand for a moment.

"Remember," Seo Myungju whispered. "They believe they have set us here to judge us. But the moment we open our mouths, the thing on trial will be baptism. They do not know that. Not yet."

As she walked, Seorin thought of Jun. The sixteen-year-old boy who had vanished into the dark, and the dog held in his arms. It was a mercy, Seorin thought, that the one caught was herself and not Jun. The boy still had many stars to see. His brother's share as well. That the seed bag was with Jun meant that the names of the erased were still alive beyond the city. Whatever should befall Seorin and Seo Myungju in here, out there someone who remembered still remained. That thought made Seorin a little less alone. The struggle of the ones who walk away was not the victory of a single person but a chain of memory that carried on without being erased. If one link broke and the next remained, the chain was not broken.

The two walked into the House of Reconciliation. Behind them the doors closed. The city gathered the ones who had left back into its arms. That embrace was warm as a grave, and dark as a grave.
