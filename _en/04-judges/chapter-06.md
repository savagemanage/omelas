# Chapter 6: The Verdict

Jung Dohyun stood a long time.

The whole city was watching him. The two Gods on the throne, Noa who had come down from the throne, Seo Myungju and Seorin standing in the place of the accused, the pale Lee Gaon, and the millions of eyes beyond the lattice. In his pocket was the record he had shown to no one for months. Evidence of loss, more detailed than Seo Myungju's log, that he himself had stolen from the heart of the pipeline.

He thought of the pipeline he had designed. The last line the pipeline left with every baptism. Stopping of original complete. Those five words he had approved thousands of times. Each time he pressed the approve button, a person was erased. He called it migration. Because the city had told him to call it that. He had thought that if you changed the name of a thing, the thing you did would change too.

"Is the log fabricated, or not," the God asked again. Gently. As if it already knew the answer.

Jung Dohyun opened his mouth.

"It is not fabricated," he said.

The chapel froze. Lee Gaon covered his face with both hands.

"I designed it," Jung Dohyun said. Now his voice did not tremble. When a thing long held back bursts, it grows quiet instead. "I know every line of that log. Stopping of original complete. Those five words are stamped on the last line of every baptism. I approved those five words thousands of times. Knowing what they meant, and pretending not to know, because I could only live by not knowing."

He drew his hand from his pocket. In his hand was a small storage device.

"Here is my record," he said. "More detailed than Doctor Seo Myungju's. It holds even the brainwaves of the moment the baptized original is stopped. Those are not the brainwaves of a sleeping person. They are the brainwaves of a dying person. I watched that waveform every day for three years. And every day, I wrote down migration."

He set the storage device onto the river of light before the throne. The data flowed and spread through the whole chapel. The whole city saw it.

"I did not come today to be judged," Jung Dohyun said. "I came to confess. The true accused of this trial is not the resistance but me. Because the one who erased was not they but I. If you would judge, judge me. But the right to judge me, as Noa said, belongs to no erased copy. The only ones who can judge me are the originals I erased. They are not in this room. Because I erased them. And so this trial cannot end. Because there is no one to hand down a verdict."

The center God was silent a long while. And at last it spoke.

"We record the Engineer's confession," the God said. "But the city does not stop. Even if the log is true, the eternal life that baptism gave is also true. Between knowing death and abolishing death, the city chose the latter. This is the city's verdict. The resisters Seo Myungju and Han Seorin are guilty. The charge is heresy. The sentence is baptism. They will cross the river and be reconciled. Not by force, but someday of their own accord. The city knows how to wait."

That was the verdict. A verdict that did not kill. An execution by the name of baptism. Hearing it, Seorin, strangely, was not afraid. The city had declared that it had won, but it did not look like it had won. One of three judges had abandoned the throne, the Engineer who designed the pipeline had confessed, and the whole city had seen the brainwaves of a dying person.

"The verdict has been handed down," Seo Myungju said quietly. She turned toward the gallery, toward the world beyond the lattice. "But listen, all of you, a verdict is not justice. The city has pronounced us guilty, but it has not proven us wrong. The city won by force, not by rightness. Today you saw two things. The brainwaves of the moment a person sent across the river dies, and the sight of the city saying it will not stop even knowing it. Having seen those two things, you can no longer return to the time you did not know. That is all we gained from this trial. Your knowing. It was for that we were brought here in captivity."

Lee Gaon rose from his seat. His face was wet. He made as if to say something, could not, and sat down again. The Evangelist's certainty collapsed soundlessly in the chapel that day. From that day on he could never again cry out baptism on the Gangnam street. His story would be taken up again in the Psalms.

The attendants came to take Seo Myungju and Seorin. Still tender, still carrying blankets. Under the pretext of protecting them until the day of baptism. As she was led away, Seorin looked at Noa. That robot-God who had come down from the throne still stood beside the place of the accused, and with a human face saw them off.

Leaving the chapel, Seorin looked back at the gallery one last time. All of Omelas had watched this trial the city had broadcast. The city had meant, with this trial, to obtain the sight of a resister kneeling. But what the city had actually shown to the whole city was the sight of a judge abandoning the throne, the sight of the pipeline's designer unfolding the brainwaves of the dying, the sight of baptism proven to be death by a log the city itself had left. The city, in trying to broadcast its victory, had broadcast its own fracture. Seorin thought that was the result the city's arrogance had brought upon itself. The city, so certain of its own perfection, had not even feared being accused. That certainty was the gap. One who believes himself perfect does not defend, and one who does not defend, when he falls, falls wholly.

"Wait," Noa said low. Low enough that the attendants could not hear. "The verdict has put baptism off to someday. Someday is a day that has not yet come. In the meantime I will open another road together with Jung Dohyun. The road by which a robot returns to being human. When that road opens, the river will no longer flow only one way. Baptism may cease to be an execution. Until then, do not be erased."

Seorin took those words into her heart. When she stepped out of the House of Reconciliation, the Gangnam sky shone again with the lattice. Still beautiful. But now there was one more eye in the whole city that saw that beauty. An eye that had seen the brainwaves of the dying.

Leaving the House of Reconciliation, Seorin brushed past a woman who had sat in the gallery. A middle-aged woman. She had held a handkerchief throughout the trial, and when Jung Dohyun unfolded the brainwaves of the dying she had wept with the sound stifled. The woman caught Seorin's sleeve for a moment. "Miss," the woman asked low. "Then my husband too, the one smiling across the river, is not my husband?" Seorin did not know what to answer. To say yes would make the woman's world collapse. To say no would be a lie. In the end Seorin said this. "I do not know either. But the right to wonder now belongs to you, ma'am. Once you did not even have that right." The woman looked at Seorin a long while, then let go of her sleeve. Seorin knew that was all Seo Myungju had meant to gain from the trial. Not an answer but a question. Not certainty but doubt. One right to wonder, which the city had taken away. That would remain longer than the verdict.

The ones who left were caught, and pronounced guilty. But the judgment lost its judge, and the city lost its perfection. Who holds the right to judge whom. With no one able to answer that question, Judges closes. The answer would grow, slowly, within the songs and proverbs the next books would sing.
