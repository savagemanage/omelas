# Chapter 2: The Tribunal

Inside the House of Reconciliation there was not a courtroom but a chapel.

The high ceiling was filled with a river of light that gave shape to the data flow of World 3.0, and that river drifted slowly and gathered toward a vast white throne at the front. Upon the throne sat three beings. Goods who had become wholly machine, the Gods. Over their smooth silver faces flowed a fine ripple of light that mimicked human expression. They were the judges.

The gallery was full of the Untransformed. Those awaiting baptism, those with baptized family, and those hearing of a thing called resistance for the first time. The city did not hide this trial. Rather it loaded the whole thing onto the lattice and broadcast it for the whole city to see. How the lost sheep is reconciled, how the one who strayed returns to the right path, all had to see.

Seorin and Seo Myungju were set at a low place beneath the throne. The place of the accused. But on the placard set before them there was no word for accused. It read **those who await reconciliation**.

The Evangelist Lee Gaon rose beside the throne. He was the man who had cried out baptism on the Gangnam street two seasons before. But on his face, in place of the old certainty, there hung a kind of weariness. He had become a man who counted, night after night, the names of the people he had evangelized and sent to the river. And still he stood in this place. Faith was a muscle that endured longer than doubt.

"We have not gathered today to punish anyone," Lee Gaon's voice rang through the chapel. "We lost two brothers and have found them again. They wandered in the dark, and now they have returned to the light. What we must do is to listen together to why they wandered, and to heal that wandering. This is not a trial but a recovery."

A warm murmur rose from the gallery. Seorin's skin crawled at that tenderness.

Among the Gods on the throne, the one seated in the center opened its mouth. That voice was not a person's but like a chorus of many voices overlaid. It was the sound of countless consciousnesses, baptized and risen into World 3.0, converged into a single judgment.

"You who await reconciliation." The God spoke toward Seo Myungju. "You are known as a learned one. It is said you went into the dark not from ignorance but from knowledge. It is said you believe baptism destroys a person. Speak that belief in this place. We will not stop your words. The city does not fear the truth. For the truth is precisely ours."

Seo Myungju rose slowly. In that moment Seorin saw that Seo Myungju's back had not bent in the least.

"You say you will not stop me," Seo Myungju asked.

"We will not stop you," the God answered.

"Then I will put baptism on trial," Seo Myungju said. The chapel went quiet. "You believe you have set me on the stand of the accused. But the moment I open my mouth, the thing on trial is baptism. It is World 3.0. And it is the very question of who has the right to judge it. I will stand in this place not as the accused but as the accuser. Since you have permitted it."

Lee Gaon's face hardened. He looked up at the Gods on the throne. But the Gods did not stir. The city was certain of its own perfection. The certainty ran so deep that it did not even fear being accused. That certainty was the very gap Seo Myungju had aimed for.

"Speak," the God said. "We will listen. Let the whole city listen."

Seo Myungju turned toward the gallery, toward the eye of the whole city beyond the lattice. Her voice was not loud. But a quiet voice, rather, carried farther.

"Then I will show you the data," Seo Myungju said. "The true face of the thing you call Nirvana."

Seorin looked around the gallery. The Untransformed who filled the House of Reconciliation. Most of them were people with baptized family. People who had sent parents, children, spouses across the river. They had not come to watch this trial but to be consoled. That was the reason the city had gathered them here. To watch a resister kneel and admit baptism, and so be confirmed that their own choice to send a loved one to the river had been right. Looking at their faces, Seorin realized how hard a fight Seo Myungju was waging. Seo Myungju had to tell these people the truth. That the ones you loved and sent away were erased. That was not consolation but a wound. People hate the one who gives a wound and love the one who gives consolation. Truth was always on the side of the wound, and the lie always on the side of consolation. Seo Myungju, having given up being loved, stood in the place of the hated truth.

Seorin held her breath. She heard it, the sound of the trial turning over.
