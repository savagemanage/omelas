# Chapter 4: The One Who Came Back

A baptized person never returns to the flesh again. That was the rule.

But in Omelas there were exceptions. Very rarely, when the migration just after baptism was unstable, the Engineers reflected the consciousness back into a temporary body terminal to observe it. People called this "Second Coming observation," and most did not even know such a procedure existed. Seorin came to know of it because of Jiwoo.

Jiwoo was Seorin's old friend. They had grown up in the same neighborhood, gone to the same school, and were among the few who shared clumsy jokes from the Old Calendar days. Jiwoo had been baptized two months ago. She had passed through the door before Seorin. And yesterday word came that she was briefly out in a body terminal, and wanted to meet.

Seorin met Jiwoo at a quiet teahouse near the chapel.

Jiwoo was Jiwoo. Her face, her voice, the way she laughed. Seorin took her hand in gladness. Though it was a temporary body terminal, the hand was warm.

"It's been a while, Seorin," Jiwoo smiled.

"Yes, it's been a while. How is it, over there?"

"Good," Jiwoo answered without hesitation. "Really good. Not a single worry. No pain, either. You should come soon."

Seorin felt a sense of déjà vu at that answer. It was the very same sentence as her parents'. Good, no worries, come soon. As if reading the same script. But it was not strange for those who had been to paradise to describe paradise alike, Seorin thought.

The two talked a long while. Jiwoo remembered the old jokes and responded exactly to the old memories Seorin threw out. The data seemed to have migrated perfectly. And yet, the longer they talked, the more Seorin began to feel a discord she could not quite grasp.

Jiwoo laughed, but the moment of laughing was minutely late. When Seorin threw out a joke, Jiwoo laughed after a very short delay. As if she laughed only after querying somewhere whether the joke was funny. The Jiwoo of the Old Calendar had been a girl who burst out laughing before Seorin even finished speaking.

And Jiwoo did not look out the window. The old Jiwoo had loved the window seat, and had always talked while looking outside. The Jiwoo now looked only at Seorin's face. Exactly, without wavering, a little too much.

"Jiwoo," Seorin suddenly asked. "Do you remember the day we ran through the sudden downpour when we were seventeen? What did you say then?"

Jiwoo laughed. After that minute delay. "Of course I remember. You said, 'This rain, it's washing us clean.'"

It was the exact answer. Too exact. A chill ran down Seorin's spine. What Jiwoo had said that day was indeed that. But when she said it, Jiwoo had been crying. It was a day she cried not over the downpour but over something else, and it was that kind of sad joke, that it was a relief the rain hid her tears. The Jiwoo now repeated that sentence in a bright voice, with no shadow at all. The words were right, but the emotion was empty.

Seorin felt her heart slowly freezing over.

"Jiwoo, you cried that day."

Jiwoo's laugh stopped for a moment. A very short pause. Then she laughed again. "Did I? I don't quite remember. But what does something like that matter. Nothing is sad now."

Nothing is sad. Whether that was proof of paradise, or proof that something had vanished, Seorin could not tell. Jiwoo held all of Jiwoo's memories. The facts, the names, the events. And yet the weight attached to those memories, the sorrow, the shadow, had been erased. As if the data remained and the person had slipped away.

As they left the teahouse, Jiwoo said one last thing. "Seorin, really, come soon. Here you don't even need to remember sad things like that. You can erase it all and be at ease."

Erase it all. Seorin's skin crawled at those words. Is it paradise to erase sorrow, or is it paradise to erase the person?

Mir met Seorin when she came home. The dog ran up wagging her tail, then suddenly stopped when she caught the scent coming off Seorin. Mir let out a low growl. She had caught Jiwoo's scent soaked into Seorin's clothes, the scent of the temporary body terminal. Mir bared her teeth not toward Seorin, but toward the door Seorin had come in through.

"Mir, what's wrong. It's me."

Mir recognized Seorin. She only refused the something clinging to her. Dogs distinguish the baptized from the Untransformed by smell, Seorin had faintly heard once. Then she had thought it superstition. Now she did not.

That night Seorin turned over Jiwoo's words again and again. You can erase it all and be at ease. To be at ease. That was surely a temptation. There were things in Seorin's life too she wished to erase. The long evenings after her parents left, the table where she ate alone, the emptiness she felt before the terminal each holiday. If she could erase all of that, how much lighter she would be. And yet when she recalled Jiwoo's face, that temptation turned cold at once. Jiwoo, who had lost the seventeen-year-old downpour, the weight of those tears. What Jiwoo lost as the price of erasing sorrow was not only sorrow. It seemed that even a certain connection with Seorin, who had gone through that sorrow with her, even a single thread that had joined the Jiwoo of that day to the Jiwoo of now, had been erased along with it. Being at ease and vanishing. Seorin wanted to know where the boundary between those two lay, and at the same time did not want to know.

That night Seorin could not sleep. Outside the window the lattice of the sky shone softly. Her parents, Jiwoo, everyone who had gone before were within it. Smiling, without sorrow, beckoning her to come. And Seorin, for the first time, was afraid of that beckoning.
