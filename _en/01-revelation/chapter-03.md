# Chapter 3: The Promise of Nirvana

On weekends the transformation chapels were crowded with people.

Seorin left Mir with a neighbor and sought out the great chapel in Gangnam. The faint discomfort that had grazed her by the river yesterday she wanted, instead, to cover over with a larger certainty. When people waver they cling to something firmer. What looked firmest to Seorin was the doctrine of baptism.

The chapel was built like a temple, but had no sacred images. Instead the entire front wall was a single vast screen, and across it flowed the inner landscape of World 3.0. A city built of light, endless meadows, rivers and mountains and seas. The baptized walked within it in bodies smoother and brighter than people. Below the screen floated a sentence. "A place without pain, without death, without parting."

Lee Gaon stepped up to the pulpit. It was the same Evangelist she had seen on the street yesterday. Inside the chapel he looked larger. The lighting stained his white robe with holiness.

"Today we speak of Nirvana," he began. "What is Nirvana? The ancients thought of it as a distant far shore reached only in death. But Omelas has drawn it down to the here and now. Through baptism we cross over while still alive."

The people held their breath.

"The flesh is a prison." Heat rose in Lee Gaon's voice. "Where does your sorrow come from? Because the body hurts. Because the body ages. Because the body of the one you love grows cold. The root of sorrow is always this lump of meat. And if you could shed that lump of meat, if you could pull up the root of sorrow, what would that be if not Nirvana?"

The woman seated beside Seorin pressed a handkerchief to the corners of her eyes. The whole chapel trembled with a single longing, and Seorin was drawn into that trembling too. Baptism. Nirvana. A place where you never hurt again, never part again. The place where her parents were.

"There are those who doubt." Lee Gaon lowered his voice. "There are those who ask whether, after baptism, I vanish and something else remains. That is the whisper of the Evils. It is the lie of those who pretend to be Awakened, who are in truth held by darkness."

Seorin heard that name for the first time. The Evils. The evil ones.

"They say baptism copies you and erases the original. That the parents you see on the screen are only copies of them." Lee Gaon paused. The chapel froze. "But who can tell that difference? If your mother recognizes you, calls your name, loves you, what is that if not your mother? What scale weighs an original and a copy differently?"

The logic was smooth. Seorin was relieved by that smoothness. Yesterday's discomfort by the river sank slowly beneath that logic. Yes, her mother was her mother. Because she recognizes me and says come on in.

"Baptism is not extinction." Lee Gaon spread both arms. "Baptism is completion. You do not vanish; you are at last made whole. Into Nirvana, into the glory of the Lord."

The woman beside Seorin folded her handkerchief and whispered to the person next to her. That last month she had sent her mother across the river. That now her mother was not sick, and how fortunate that was. In that voice there was a heartfelt relief. Seorin envied that relief. That woman believed without wavering. That her mother had entered Nirvana. That belief put the woman at ease. Seorin wanted to believe that way too. That her parents were truly at peace within it. But what the old woman by the river had said kept coming back. The dogs seem to know. What we don't. Seorin tried to shake off that sentence and sink deeper into Lee Gaon's sermon. When people waver they cling to a louder sound. But no matter how she clung, in one corner of her heart the quiet moment when Mir would not wag her tail before the screen kept reviving.

When the service ended, people lined up before the counseling desks. It was the line for reserving a baptism date. Seorin stood at the end of that line too. Her heart pounded. At the end of this line were her parents. There was completion.

As the line shrank, a white-robed attendant approached and handed Seorin a pamphlet. On the sleek paper were written the steps of baptism. Preliminary examination, consciousness scan, data migration, and completion. Beside the last item was a note in small print.

"After migration, the original flesh is recovered and enshrined."

Seorin snagged on that sentence. Recovered. Enshrined. Those words were not the sentences of paradise. They had, somewhere, the smell of a funeral.

"Excuse me," Seorin asked the attendant. "The flesh... what happens to it? This being enshrined."

The attendant answered with a trained smile. "It's just a shell. Don't worry. We tend to it with dignity."

A shell. The very same metaphor as Lee Gaon's. Seorin nodded, but one corner of her heart scraped again. If it's a shell, why tend to it with dignity? If it were truly nothing, why recover and enshrine it?

Seorin recalled her grandmother's funeral in childhood. Then people had not called her grandmother's body a shell. They washed that body, dressed it in fine clothes, and held it a long time and wept. People do not hold a shell like that and weep. If people tend to something with such care, it means that something is precious. If baptism truly regarded the body as a worthless husk, the very rite of enshrinement would be unnecessary. It would be enough to burn it and forget. And yet the city recovered those bodies and enshrined them somewhere. The bodies of people said to have crossed over into paradise, deliberately. Seorin felt as if she were touching the seam of that contradiction with her fingertips. It was a very small seam, but once touched, her hand kept going back to it.

She did not reserve baptism that day. Instead she only folded the pamphlet, put it in her bag, and left the chapel. She meant to tell her parents, not yet. That she would think a little more.

Someone was watching Seorin as she left the chapel. A woman in a gray coat leaned against a pillar, looking back and forth between the line at the baptism counseling desk and Seorin, who had come out of that line alone. In the woman's eyes there was a light exactly opposite to Lee Gaon's. It was the light of a warning, not an invitation.

Seorin had not yet noticed that gaze. But the woman had marked Seorin. Because Seorin was one of the few who had stepped out of the line.
