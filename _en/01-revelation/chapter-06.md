# Chapter 6: Revelation

For three days Seorin did not look at the storage device.

She put it deep in a drawer and passed before it every day. If she did not look, it seemed she could go back to before. She could believe her parents' smiles, Jiwoo's greeting, the lattice of the sky, as she once had. Seo Myungju had been right. The comfortable lie was truly comfortable.

But on the fourth night, Seorin opened the drawer.

When she connected the storage device to her terminal, the screen filled with data. Seorin was no Scientist, but she could recognize the points Seo Myungju had marked. The consciousness data before baptism and the data after lay side by side. On the surface the two were identical. The structure of memory, the pattern of personality, the habits of language, all matched. It looked like a perfect migration.

But at every place Seo Myungju had marked in red, there was a minute fissure.

It was a layer of emotion. In the data before baptism, each memory carried a complexly tangled grain of feeling. In the data after baptism, that layer had thinned. The memory remained, but the weight of contradiction that had wrapped it was smoothly ironed out. Where sorrow and joy had mingled, only a tidy peace remained.

It was Jiwoo's downpour. That complex sorrow of hiding tears in the rain, in the later data, had been pressed into a single tidy layer called "a pleasant memory." In the place where sorrow had vanished, peace remained, and that peace the city called Nirvana.

At the very end of the file was a short note Seo Myungju had left.

"Baptism does not kill a person. It is worse than that. It erases the person, seats a smiling copy in their place, and then makes it so no one knows they have died. Not even the bereaved. Since there is no one to grieve, there is no funeral either. A perfect city has not abolished death; it has abolished the people who grieve death. Omelas is a paradise built not upon a single sacrifice, but upon the quiet extinction of everyone baptized each day. And that extinction we call a blessing, and we line up for it."

Seorin turned off the screen. The room grew dark. Outside the window the lattice of World 3.0 shone like an aurora. Her parents were within it. Jiwoo was within it. Everyone who had gone before.

No. What was within it was not her parents. It was a copy of her parents. Her real parents had already been erased three years ago in the moment of baptism. The smile that beckoned her to come on the screen was not her parents' smile but the smile of a city that had erased her parents. And that city truly loved Seorin. Truly meant to erase Seorin too.

Seorin wept a long time. Without a sound. As Jiwoo had hidden her tears in the rain that day, Seorin too hid her tears in the darkness.

Seorin read again and again the note Seo Myungju had left at the end of the file. A perfect city has not abolished death; it has abolished the people who grieve death. Seorin stopped a long while before that sentence. She recalled the day her parents were baptized. That day Seorin had not grieved. She had celebrated. Because she had been taught to rejoice when parents enter Nirvana. She had held no funeral for her parents. Because there was no funeral. She had only smiled and seen them off, saying, go well, we'll meet across the river. Now Seorin knew. What she had celebrated that day was the death of her parents. She had smiled where she should have grieved, applauded where she should have mourned. What the city had taken from her was not only her parents. It had taken even her own heart to grieve her parents. Only after three years could Seorin, for the first time, weep for her parents. Tears three years late.

Mir came near and licked Seorin's hand. The dog's tongue was warm, and the dog's eyes were alive. Mir could not read data, but she had known from the start. From the moment she laid her ears flat toward Lee Gaon on the Gangnam street, from the moment she would not wag her tail at the parents on the screen. The living from the not-living, a dog always knew.

Then the terminal rang. It was her mother's signal. This late at night.

With trembling hands Seorin accepted the screen. Her mother was smiling. As always, with a smooth and peaceful face.

"Seorin, still awake?" her mother said. "Evangelist Lee Gaon contacted us earlier. That you left the chapel without reserving baptism. And that lately you've been meeting a bad person. Seorin, beware the Evils. They are after your soul."

Seorin looked at her mother on the screen. Her mother knew that Seorin had met Seo Myungju. Lee Gaon had told her, and Lee Gaon had been told by the city. The city did not forget the one who stepped out of the line. The city was watching.

"Mom," Seorin asked. Her voice was strangely calm. "Did you ever get caught in a downpour when you were seventeen?"

Her mother on the screen laughed. After that minute delay. "What are you talking about, all of a sudden. Come now, it's late. Reserve your baptism soon and come over. Here nothing is sad. You can erase it all and be at ease."

Erase it all. The very same sentence as Jiwoo's. The same script as her parents'. The voice of the city.

Seorin looked a long while at her mother's smooth face on the screen. And for the first time, she resolved to flee from paradise. She did not yet fully know where to go, or what was right. But one thing was clear. That she would not walk into that smooth smile.

"Mom," Seorin said quietly. "Sleep well."

She cut the connection. The screen went dark. The lattice outside the window still shone, but now it looked not like the sky of paradise but like a vast mouth quietly swallowing people every day.

Mir barked low toward the door. Seorin followed the dog's gaze. On the street below the window, two white-robed attendants stood looking up at Seorin's house. This late at night.

The city had come to fetch the lost sheep. Seorin began to pack a bag. Her hands trembled, but she did not stop.

Seorin did not know what to put in the bag. One who has never left does not know what to pack. She put in whatever her hands found. Mir's leash and a few bags of dog food. The old handkerchief her mother had used in the Old Calendar days. The storage device Seo Myungju had given her. And things of no use at all, one childhood photograph, one blurry photo taken with Jiwoo. As she packed, Seorin realized that she was not leaving the city but gathering the people the city had erased. The real parents, the real Jiwoo, the people from before they became copies. To remember them was now the only funeral Seorin had left.

Mir barked low again. The sound of the doorbell followed. Seorin paused her hands a moment and looked down at the white robes below the window. They too had surely come to save her in earnest. With the face of a shepherd worried for a lost sheep. That was the most terrifying thing about this city. That even those who came to erase a person came wearing the face of love.

The revelation was over. Now the leaving remained. But that was the next story.

Outside, one of the white-robed attendants pressed the doorbell.
