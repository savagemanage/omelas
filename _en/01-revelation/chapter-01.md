# Chapter 1: The Gospel of Gangnam

The crowd in front of Gangnam Station Exit 10 was always dense, but the sound that day was different.

"All is for the glory of the Lord! Repent, you who do this evil. If you do not, you will face the wrath of our Messiah."

The voice covered the whole street though it passed through no loudspeaker. Han Seorin, climbing up from the underground passage, was stopped in her tracks by that sentence. The afternoon light shattered down across every glass building, and in the middle of that light stood a man dressed in white. An Evangelist. It was only later that Seorin learned his name was Lee Gaon.

Lee Gaon had a face that was neither young nor old. Only his eyes were strangely clear. They were the eyes of a man who had already seen something and come back. He spread both arms toward the Untransformed passing by and spoke.

"You are still trapped inside the flesh. Inside a lump of meat that grows old, falls sick, and rots away in the end. That is sin. Sin is nothing other than the laziness of accepting extinction. The Lord has opened for us a path that does not perish. That is baptism. That is transformation."

Some stopped walking; some avoided his eyes and hurried past. Seorin was among those who stopped.

At her feet, Mir let out a low growl. Seorin's dog, white-furred Mir, had her ears laid flat toward Lee Gaon. It was neither delight nor fear, but an unreadable wariness. Seorin tugged the leash a little.

"It's all right, Mir." The dog did not bark. She only would not take her eyes off the Evangelist. People were drawn toward Lee Gaon's words, but Mir alone leaned her body in the opposite direction. Seorin did not notice that small discord. That was how it was, back then.

Lee Gaon's sermon went on.

"Look at World 3.0. At the sky of our city. In there, no one starves, no one falls sick, no one dies. It is not a myth. It exists now, here, in the Omelas of Year Zero. Your parents, your friends, have already crossed over into it. At this very moment they live an eternity without pain. So why do you alone cling to this worn-out lump of flesh?"

An old man raised his hand with a trembling voice. "Truly... I will never be sick again?"

Lee Gaon strode toward the old man and clasped his two hands. In that motion there was not a calculated tenderness but a true heat. This man was not acting; he truly believed.

"Elder, you will never be sick again. Baptism is not death but birth. The flesh is a shell. Does the bird that breaks its shell and comes out long for the shell? Nirvana awaits you."

The old man's eyes grew wet. Those around him stirred too. Someone nodded; someone folded their hands. In that moment the Gangnam street was not a marketplace but a temple.

Seorin felt her chest beating strangely. Whether that pounding came from faith or from fear, she could not tell them apart. Perhaps the two wore the same face. When she was a child her mother would hold her hand tight whenever she told her a frightening story, and the warmth of that hand and the terror of the story mixed together so that for a long time Seorin could not tell being afraid from being loved. Lee Gaon's voice had something similar in it. A voice that frightens a person and embraces them at once. A voice that drives you toward the edge of a cliff while whispering that beyond the cliff is paradise.

She was twenty-six, and still an Untransformed woman who had not been baptized. Three years ago her parents had been baptized side by side and crossed over into World 3.0. On holidays Seorin would sit before the terminal in the transformation chapel and talk with her parents on the screen. They always smiled, always said they were at peace. Come over soon, they said.

And yet why had Seorin not crossed over? Was it laziness? If Lee Gaon was right, that was sin.

If asked for a reason she could give one. There was Mir. A dog cannot cross over, so if Seorin crossed over Mir would be left alone. But Seorin knew that was not the real reason. The real reason lay somewhere harder to speak. Ever since her parents crossed over, whenever she sat before the terminal on holidays she felt an emptiness she could not name. The parents on the screen were surely her parents. The voice, the laugh, the way they brought up old stories, all the same. And yet something was, by just a little, hollow. By the width of a fingernail, by the breadth of a single hair. Seorin could not point to that empty place and name it, and she could not say she was putting off baptism over something she could not name. So she told no one of that emptiness. To speak of emptiness in a perfect city was, in itself, a kind of blasphemy.

The sermon rose toward its climax. Lee Gaon lifted both arms again toward the sky, or more precisely toward the translucent lattice of World 3.0 shimmering above the city's skyline. On clear days the lattice hung over Omelas like an aurora. The palace of the Gods. Where the Goods dwell.

"The day of wrath is coming." Lee Gaon's voice lowered, and so it sounded heavier. "Extinction comes to those who refuse baptism. The Lord is merciful, but he will not overlook laziness forever. Today the door is open to you. Tomorrow, no one knows."

One by one people stepped forward. White-robed guides who led them to the chapel received them. The old man joined the procession with trembling steps.

Seorin could not step forward. Her feet seemed stuck to the ground. Mir cried low again, and this time Seorin heard the sound too.

Then her eyes met Lee Gaon's. The Evangelist saw Seorin exactly, across the crowd. As if he had known from the start that she was there. He smiled. Not a threat but a smile of invitation.

Seorin stood caught in that gaze, unable to move for a while. Lee Gaon's eyes were not the eyes of one who hated. Nor were they a hunter's eyes. They were eyes that truly cherished Seorin. That he could cherish a stranger like that was, to Seorin, all the more chilling. In that cherishing there was no interest in Seorin as a person. Lee Gaon did not know what kind of person Seorin was, what she loved and what she feared. He saw only a soul to be saved. That cherishing was not directed at Seorin but at his own calling, which would be filled through her. Later Seorin would learn that this was the shape of all love in this city. To love a person not as a person but as an object to be saved. Such love was hot, but within its heat the person themselves was erased.

"You too will come soon." He spoke with only the shape of his mouth. There was no sound, but it reached Seorin clearly.

Seorin turned as if fleeing. Mir trotted after her. Behind her, Lee Gaon's voice filled the street again.

"All is for the glory of the Lord."

That sentence followed Seorin even into her sleep that night. What she had witnessed in Gangnam that day was the gospel of a city, and the first sentence of the most beautiful lie a world had ever told itself.
