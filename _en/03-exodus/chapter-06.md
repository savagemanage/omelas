# Chapter 6: The Ones Who Walk Away

Outside there was no road.

In the place drawn only in gray on the map there was truly nothing. No lattice, no chapel, no Evangelist's voice. Only old fields and abandoned old roads and ruins the city had forgotten. And there were stars. The real sky the city had been hiding.

The five walked all night. As dawn drew near, they reached the top of a hill and stopped and looked back. Omelas, its sky covered by the lattice, still shone like an aurora even in the dawn light. That beauty was no lie. The only lie was upon what that beauty was built.

"There was a person called Le Guin," Seo Myungju said, looking down at the city. "A person from before the Old Calendar. She wrote a story about a city called Omelas. Our city's name came from there. David gave it, they say. That it was the name of the most perfect utopia he knew."

"What kind of story was it?" Seorin asked.

"A story about a perfect city. A city where no one starves and everyone is happy. But that happiness was built upon a single condition. In some basement room of the city, a child is locked away, starving and weeping in the dark. All the happiness of the city is built upon that child's misery. When people grow up they come to learn the fact, and most, though they know, remain in the city. Persuading themselves that if this one child's misery lets so many people be happy, it is a bearable price."

Seorin looked at the city behind her. In this city there was not one child in a basement room. Every person who crossed the river each day, the originals erased and replaced, unmourned, by smiling copies, was that child.

"But there are others in that story too," Seo Myungju went on. "A very small number. Those who, after seeing the child in the basement, pack their things without a word and walk out one night through the city's gate. They don't know where they're going. The story doesn't even say what's outside. And yet they walk out. Those people, the story calls them this. The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas."

The dawn wind came over the hill. Jun stood beside Seorin holding Mir, and the remaining two came near. Five stood on the hilltop and looked down at the perfect city they had left.

"We're those people," Seorin said. It was not a question.

"Whether the ones who walk away in that story were right, I don't know," Seo Myungju said honestly. "Their leaving doesn't free the child in the basement either. They only refuse to be happy upon that misery. Our leaving won't stop the city's baptisms either. That the five of us walked out, perhaps it's nothing."

Jun, holding Mir, looked up at the stars and asked. "How will we live now, ma'am? There's nothing here." Seorin could not answer the boy's question at once. It was true. Outside there was no home, no food, no safety. Had they stayed in the city they would have lived warm and full, without a single worry. Even with the day of baptism on their backs. Seorin could not be sure it was right to have thrown away that comfort and chosen this cold, empty field. But Seorin answered the boy this way. "I don't know. It'll be hard. We'll be hungry and cold. But Jun, from now on we'll live a life that's ours. Not a life the city set for us. Even if it's hard, it's ours. Rather than that life that was comfortable but wasn't ours, I think this life that's hard but is ours is better. Maybe I'm wrong. But that's something we get to decide by living it. That too was something we couldn't do before."

"Then why leave?" Jun asked.

Seo Myungju looked at the boy a long while. "To grieve. The city abolished sorrow. Because without sorrow, it looks as though nothing is wrong. We've come out to a place where we can grieve. To grieve Taesik, your brother, Seorin's parents. That sorrow is the proof that we're still human. So our leaving isn't meaningless. We came out to reclaim the right to grieve."

Seorin looked at the seed bag Seo Myungju had held to her chest all night. Inside it were the data and stories of the erased, the testimonies of unmourned deaths. They would plant it somewhere out here, among people the lattice had not yet reached. Whether that seed would grow, whether the city would someday fall, nothing could be known.

"Will we win?" Seorin asked.

"Probably not. At least not in our lifetime," Seo Myungju said.

"And yet we go?"

"And yet we go."

Seorin nodded. That answer strangely put her heart at ease. The city had always promised something certain. Certain happiness, certain peace, certain Nirvana. That certainty was the lie. The uncertainty Seo Myungju promised was, rather, honest. Living things were always uncertain, and the only certain things were the erased.

Seorin looked again at the seed bag. What Taesik had traded his life to protect. What it held was not a weapon. Not a bomb, not code to bring down the city. Only names. The names of the erased, and records of what kind of people they had been. A person who was curt, a person who cried easily, a person who enjoyed bad jokes. The uneven, real faces the city had covered over with smooth copies. Only now did Seorin understand that the one weapon the Evils held was memory. The city had erased people by erasing sorrow. So resistance was not some grand thing but this very thing, to remember and grieve the erased. As long as one person remained who grieved, the erased were not completely erased.

The sun began to rise. The first light spread over the field beyond the hill. Mir pricked her nose toward that light, and for the first time, in a place without the city's eye, barked loud.

The five turned their backs on the city. They did not know where they were going. Hardship surely awaited. Hunger, cold, regret. All the things the city had abolished. But along with all of it, the freedom to grieve, the freedom to be alive, would await.

They walked. Toward where the stars faded and the sun rose. The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas, toward a morning where nothing was guaranteed. Behind them the city glittered one last time. Come back soon. Here nothing is sad.

No one looked back.

The Exodus was not over. It had only just begun. And the city would never forget those who had left. The city would pursue them. It would try to set them on trial. But that was the next story.
