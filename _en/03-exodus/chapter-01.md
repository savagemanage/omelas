# Chapter 1: The Doorbell

The doorbell rang twice.

Seorin stood before the door with her bag slung over her shoulder, holding her breath. Beyond the door came a soft voice. The white-robed attendants never knocked. They pressed the doorbell, waited, and called the name in a voice full of love.

"Miss Han Seorin. I'm sorry for the late hour. I've come at Evangelist Lee Gaon's request. I'll just see your face and go."

Seorin did not answer. Still wearing her shoes, she crossed the room to the narrow window behind the kitchen. Many unbaptized people lived in this old building, and the buildings of the unbaptized always kept a back way the city had not quite swallowed. Seorin opened the window and stepped down onto the low wall of the flowerbed. Mir followed without a sound. The dog did not bark. The dog knew that now was not a time to bark.

Behind her the attendant's voice called the name once more. Still tender. That it did not grow angry was, rather, frightening. Time was infinite to the city, and the city knew that the fled sheep, worn out in the end, returns to the riverside of its own accord.

Climbing over the wall, Seorin thought for a moment about what she was throwing away now. This house. The house where the three of them had lived together before her parents were baptized. On the wall still remained the pencil marks measuring her childhood height, and in the kitchen were the old dishes her mother had used. Seorin was leaving all of it behind. Yet strangely her regret was not great. That house had long since emptied. Ever since her parents crossed the river, it had become not a place where people lived but a place where absence lived. Those evenings sitting alone before the terminal on holidays, talking with parents on the screen. Seorin now knew that those evenings had in fact been funerals, only that no one had called them funerals. So she was not leaving a house, but at last stepping out of a funeral hall whose service had ended long ago.

The alley was dark, but not completely dark. No alley in Gangnam was ever completely dark. The lattice of the sky flowed down like an aurora, draping a pale net pattern over the wet asphalt. That light was beautiful. Now that Seorin knew what that beauty covered, she felt nauseous every time she saw it.

Seorin recalled Seo Myungju's words. Handing her the storage device behind the chapel, with that calm face, Seo Myungju had said this. "When you resolve someday to flee, come to a place where things from before the Old Calendar remain. To a place where people still remember death. There you'll be able to find us." The farther one moved from the city's center, the sparser the lattice grew. At the southern edge of the city, in the alleys of an old market where redevelopment had been postponed, people called it the old town. A place where people who still grieved death lived. Seorin walked toward it.

Those walking the night streets were mostly people not yet erased. The baptized had no need of night. In the darkness they had no worry to toss over, no regret that kept them from sleep. An old man crouched before a convenience store eating instant noodles, a young man drunk and leaning on a wall, a woman walking pushing a stroller. Three days ago Seorin would have pitied them as people not yet saved. Now Seorin knew. Those weary faces were the very ones still alive. Sorrow was proof of being alive, and the city, by abolishing sorrow, had abolished people.

Mir suddenly stopped walking and looked back. The dog's ears stood up.

Seorin looked back too. Far off, at the mouth of the alley, stood a single white robe. Not hurrying, not running, only walking quietly along the path Seorin had come. The attendant had not lost Seorin. The city's eye was the lattice, and the lattice covered the sky.

Seorin quickened her pace toward the side where the lattice was sparse. Off the main road into an alley, from the alley into a narrower alley. Mir led the way, choosing the path. The dog knew the way by smell better than a person, and by instinct found the paths where the baptized had not set foot. After two, three turns the white robe behind her disappeared from sight. But Seorin did not relax. Not seen did not mean not there. The sky was still shining.

When she had walked an hour, the lattice noticeably thinned. The net of the sky grew sparse, and through it a few dim stars showed. The smell of the air changed. The smell of old wood and oil and wet earth. Not the smell of death, but the smell of living things growing old.

At the entrance of the market stood rows of faded awnings. Most had closed their doors, but a small noodle shop in one corner had its lights on. On an old wooden chair in front of it sat a woman, writing something in a notebook spread on her knee. It was Seo Myungju.

Seo Myungju spoke without lifting her head. "You came sooner than I thought."

Seorin could not find words to answer. Between the self of three days ago and the self of now, too many things had collapsed.

Seo Myungju closed the notebook and looked up at Seorin. "I knew you'd come. A person who has seen the truth ends up either fleeing or shutting their own eyes, one of the two. The ones who shut their eyes don't come here." She looked for a moment past Seorin, at the sky where the lattice had thinned. "Someone followed you."

"One," Seorin barely said. "I think I shook them, but I'm not sure."

"Nothing is sure. From now on, nothing." Seo Myungju rose and nodded toward the dark door inside the noodle shop. "Come inside. There are people to introduce. More people are awake than you think. It's only that none of them had the courage to flee."

Just before she crossed the threshold, Seorin paused a moment and looked back. The city she had lived in for twenty-six years spread out beneath that light. The city her parents had crossed the river in, the city where Jiwoo had smiled, the city where she sat before the terminal on holidays. Throwing all of it away, Seorin was now about to walk into the darkness. With the fear came a strange sense of relief. She could not go back to before she knew the truth, but at least now she was walking while knowing. With her eyes open. Whether that was better than being led to paradise with eyes shut, she could not be sure, but Seorin had already decided not to choose the shutting of her eyes.

Seorin stepped inside the door with Mir. Behind her the light of the noodle shop went out. The lattice of the sky draped its net pattern one last time, then released Seorin into the shadow beneath the eaves.
