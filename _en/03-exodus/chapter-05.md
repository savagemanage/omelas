# Chapter 5: The Price

The storm drain was dark and damp. Seven people walked through it in a single file. Jun led the way, feeling out the path he had walked with his brother by memory, Seorin in the middle holding Mir, and Taesik at the very back bearing the seed bag. The dog did not bark even in the narrow, dark pipe. She only now and then growled low and pricked her nose toward the darkness ahead.

The lattice did not reach inside the pipe. The waterway was one of the few seams the city's eye could not reach. The complete darkness without the net of the sky was a freedom felt for the first time in a long while. In the darkness Seorin drew a deep breath for the first time.

"My brother said," Jun spoke low from up front, "that if you come out the end of this pipe, you can see a whole lot of stars. Because there's no lattice. My brother and I promised we'd go out to the outside together. But my brother went to be baptized." The boy's voice wavered in the dark. "So I have to see them for my brother's share too. The stars."

Seorin spoke quietly into the darkness. "Let's see them together. All of us."

Taesik said nothing from the very back. In the dark Seorin heard only his breathing. He was a man who had sent his wife to baptism three years ago. Even after joining the Evils he seldom spoke of himself, but Seorin remembered a word he had let slip once in the back room of the noodle shop. That his wife had smiled even on the morning of her baptism. As if she were just going out. As if she were going on a trip. And that the wife who came back on the screen still smiled, but in that smile Taesik could not find his wife. That he turned on the terminal every night and talked with his wife, and every night grew a little more certain that it was not his wife. Recalling that story, Seorin faintly understood what filled Taesik's silence in the dark.

The pipe was long. The farther they walked, the deeper the water grew. From ankles to calves, from calves to knees. The southern sewage of the city flowed into this old waterway. Seo Myungju stopped. "Why is the water rising like this?"

Then from behind Taesik said low. "A light."

Everyone looked back. From the mouth of the pipe they had entered, a pale light was approaching. It was the lanterns of the white-robed attendants. The path Jun and his brother alone had said they knew, the city had found out in the end. Even the seams the lattice did not reach, after watching long enough.

"A sluice gate," Jun said urgently. "The city has opened a sluice gate. They're filling it with water to flush us out. We have to run."

They ran. Through the water rising to their knees, to their waists. Bearing the seed bag, holding the dog, gripping one another's hands. The light behind them drew nearer. From ahead came the faint smell of the night air. The end of the pipe was not far.

When the water reached their chests, Taesik stopped.

"The bag mustn't get wet," he said. He held the bag with the seed above his head. "This one thing has to be saved. Go on ahead, all of you."

"Come with us," Seorin cried.

"The one who smiles in there isn't my wife," Taesik laughed in a voice barely audible over the sound of the water. "I lost my wife three years ago already. I've nothing left to lose now. Miss Seorin, you still have someone to grieve for. Someone to grieve has to stay alive. Go."

Taesik turned and threw the seed bag to Seo Myungju. Seo Myungju caught it and held it. And Taesik walked, against the current, toward the approaching light. Toward the white-robed attendants. To draw their gaze, the city's eye, onto himself. So the people behind could get out.

Seorin tried not to look back but looked back. In the pale light, Taesik stood with both arms spread, as he had held his wife long ago at their wedding. The attendants did not seize him. They only tenderly held out their hands. Come. Your wife is waiting. Taesik cried out something. Buried in the sound of water it could not be heard, but Seorin thought it was about why his wife had cried at the wedding. The name of the sorrow the city had erased. To the moment of death he refused to be erased.

They spilled out the end of the pipe.

Seorin braced her knees on the muddy bank and retched up water. Seo Myungju sank down, clutching the seed bag to her chest, and Jun held the dog and wept. Mir, shivering in her wet body, stayed still in the boy's arms. Five people remained. Taesik had saved them.

Seorin looked up at the sky.

There was no lattice. The net of the sky, the aurora-like light, the vast mouth that swallowed people every day, was gone. In its place was a real night sky. Black, deep, full of stars. The stars Jun had promised to see with his brother. The boy lifted his wet face and looked at the sky. He wept and laughed.

"Brother," Jun said. "Do you see? There really are a lot."

Seo Myungju came and sat beside Seorin. She said nothing a long while, then spoke quietly. "Whether we're right, whether it was right to leave Taesik behind, where those stars will take us, I still don't know. I only know one thing. We didn't walk into that smile. That one thing."

Jun looked up at the sky a long while, holding the dog. The boy said he had promised to see these stars with his brother. Now his brother was gone. His brother had been erased, smiling, at the riverside of baptism. But the boy had come this far along the waterway his brother had shown him, and was at last seeing the stars his brother had longed to see. The path his brother left had saved the boy. Watching this, Seorin thought. Even the erased leave something behind. Jun's brother left the waterway, Taesik left the time to save the seed bag, and Seorin's parents left Seorin with Mir. The city erased people, but it could not erase what those people left behind. To inherit what was left and to go on living was another way of remembering the erased. Looking at the stars, Seorin for the first time wept quietly for Taesik. Under a sky where she could grieve. Under a sky without the lattice.

Seorin looked back at the city behind her. Omelas, its sky covered by the lattice, was shining. A perfect paradise without death or sorrow. Her parents were in there, Jiwoo was in there, and Taesik would go there now. The city still loved them truly. Truly, as it erased them.
