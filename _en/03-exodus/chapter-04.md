# Chapter 4: A Pursuit Called Love

Lee Gaon did not hate Seorin. That was the problem.

The Evangelist stood before the noodle shop in the old district. Four white-robed attendants stood behind him, and beside him an Engineer read the signals of the lattice on a terminal. Lee Gaon gazed a long while at the darkened lights of the noodle shop. On his face there was no anger. What there was, was a heartfelt sorrow for a lost sheep.

"Sister Seorin is near here," the Engineer said. A young woman. Her voice was businesslike. "There's a record of access to baptism data under Untransformed family rights. The location breaks off here. It's a district where the lattice is thin. There's a strong chance she slipped into a storm drain or an old waterway."

Lee Gaon nodded. "Don't chase her. If you rush, she'll take fright," he said. "A frightened sheep will leap even off a cliff. We are guides. Not hunters. We only open the way and let the child come back of her own accord."

The Engineer glanced at him. "Evangelist. The city's order is retrieval. There's no leisure to wait for her to come back on her own. If those people carry baptism data to the outside, the Untransformed beyond the lattice will be shaken. That's a loss the city can't bear."

"Loss. You call people a loss," Lee Gaon repeated the word.

The Engineer was silent a moment. Her name was Hayun. She was a young technician who managed the baptism pipeline under Jung Dohyun, and she knew Jung Dohyun had grown strange lately. That he kept looking for loss in the data, gazing at traces of collapse in the migrated minds.

"I don't call people a loss," Hayun said. "I call data a loss. People, once baptized, are saved. That's what I was taught."

"I believe that too," Lee Gaon said. For the first time there was a minute waver in his voice. "But Hayun. Something strange has been happening lately. People I preached to and led to baptism contact me from beyond the screen. To thank me. To say they were saved because of me. And they all speak in exactly the same manner, with exactly the same smile. When they were alive they were all different. Some were curt, some even hated me. But after crossing the river they all love me the same way. If Nirvana truly makes us one, is that a blessing, or has it erased the ways we were each different?"

Hayun could not answer. It was the same question Jung Dohyun kept asking these days.

Lee Gaon stood before the locked door of the noodle shop and spoke low, as if Seorin were beyond the door.

"Sister Seorin. I haven't come to catch you. I've come to show you the way. Your parents pray for you in there every day. Come soon. Here nothing is sad. I know you're afraid. But Sister Seorin, fear is a sickness. We can cure that sickness. You can live without fear, without sorrow, without death. Why is that a bad thing?"

Seorin did not hear those words. She was already inside the storm drain. But if she had heard them, she would have known that the words were sincere, and were therefore all the more frightening.

Lee Gaon stood a long while before the door. Then he turned and said to Hayun. "Find the waterway. But don't drive her. Only watch. I want to see where the child goes. Perhaps," he paused a moment, "perhaps the child is right."

Hayun looked at him in surprise. It was not something to come from an Evangelist's mouth. Hayun thought of Jung Dohyun. Her superior lately pored over the pipeline logs all night, searching for something no one else could see. Now even the Evangelist wavers. The two people who handled baptism most closely in the city, the hand that designed it and the mouth that sang it, trembled together before the same question. Hayun feared that trembling would spread to her. She still wished to believe. If her belief wavered, she had no confidence to bear the question of what all the baptisms she had managed, all the people she had sent across the river, would become.

Lee Gaon seemed startled at what he had just said. He hurriedly added, "No. That can't be. We're right. We're saving people." But the words were not as firm as before. They were not a certainty, but a prayer to be certain.

As he walked, Lee Gaon recalled the streets of Gangnam long ago. The day he first cried out baptism. Then there had not been a speck of doubt in his heart. He was a shepherd who drew people out of death, and that calling made him shine. When people knelt before his sermon, he believed the Lord worked through his voice. That certainty was sweet. The one who is certain sleeps well at night. Because there is no question. Now Lee Gaon did not sleep well at night. The faces he had seen off to the river came to him in dreams. Faces that thanked him with the same smile from within the screen. That sameness woke him. The one who has lost certainty tosses at night. But Lee Gaon, strangely, felt that this now when he slept poorly was more human than the days when he slept well. A night tossing with a question was more honest than a night asleep in certainty. He knew he was wavering, and did not yet know whether to call that wavering a sin or a grace.

The night deepened. Under the sky of the old district where the lattice had thinned, the Evangelist and the Engineer walked toward the waterway. They pursued the fled people in the name of love, in the name of order. They were not evil. That was what made this pursuit most frightening. What chased Seorin was not a monster but people who truly wished to save her. The road that leads to hell was opened by such a tender voice.
