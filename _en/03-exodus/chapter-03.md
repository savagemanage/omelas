# Chapter 3: The Weight of Proof

Gathering proof resembled a funeral.

Over several days, following the method Seo Myungju had taught her, Seorin accessed her parents' baptism data. The Untransformed family still had the right to view the records of the baptized. The city called it a loving consideration, and Seo Myungju called it the city's arrogance. Having made it so there was no one to grieve, the city believed there would be no one to dig into the data either.

Her parents' data settled onto Seorin's terminal. Before and after baptism, side by side. When Seo Myungju ran an old analysis tool, two curves overlapped on the screen. On the surface the two lay folded into one. The map of memory, the pattern of personality, the grain of the voice, all matched.

"Here," Seo Myungju said, magnifying one point. "Emotional density. Before baptism, multiple emotions are tangled contradictorily around a single memory. Happy and sad, loving and resentful. Human memory is originally this messy. After baptism that density is pressed down, so only one emotion remains around one memory. The city calls this purification, and we call it amputation."

Seorin opened the memory of the day her father first held her. In the data before baptism, fear was mixed into that memory. The fear, trembling, that he must protect this small thing, that he might not become a good father. In the data after baptism that fear had vanished, and only a smooth, peaceful, complete joy remained.

"My father was afraid as he held me. That was my real father," Seorin said quietly. "The thing in there now that smiles saying it loves me is something that doesn't know how to be afraid."

"That's the proof," Seo Myungju said. "A human carries fear and love together. Cut out only the fear, and the love stops being real too. That is a copy of love, not love."

But data alone was not enough. What people understand is not a curve but a story. So the Evils gathered testimonies along with the data. Taesik's wife, Jun's brother, Seorin's parents. The data was the bone of the story, and the story was the flesh of the data.

They bound all of it into a single bundle. They divided it among several old storage devices. So that if one were seized, another would survive. Seo Myungju called it the seed. "If we plant this seed beyond the lattice," she said, "someday it will grow. Even if we don't see it, the next person will."

The problem was the carrying.

At the southern edge of the city, past the old district, was the boundary of Omelas. The place where the lattice completely ended. A gray zone with nothing drawn on the map. The city called it the outside, and said nothing about the outside. The baptized did not wonder about the outside. The worry to wonder had been erased. Only the Untransformed occasionally thought of the outside, and mostly folded the thought away in fear.

"There are checkpoints at the boundary," Taesik said, spreading a paper map from the Old Calendar days. The paper map still remembered the old roads the city had forgotten. "The official passages have scanners. They read whether you're baptized, and if an Untransformed person tries to leave, they catch you. The city hates people leaving. Those who leave come back and talk about the outside."

"Then where do we get out?" Seorin asked.

Jun pointed to one spot on the map. A place where an old stream crossed the city's boundary. Now covered over and turned into a road. "Here, a storm drain. My brother and I played here as kids. The lattice doesn't reach it. Because it's a waterway. If you follow this, you can pass beneath the checkpoint and get out to the outside."

Seo Myungju looked at the boy a long while. "Are you the only one who knows this path?"

Jun nodded. "My brother told me. Only my brother and I knew. Now only I do."

The basement fell quiet again. The only escape route the boy knew was the last gift left by the brother he had lost. The city had erased his brother, but it could not erase the path his brother had given the boy. The things the erased people had left behind were now guiding them out beyond the city.

A plan was set. They would divide and carry the seed, and three nights later, on the night of the dark moon, cross the boundary along the storm drain Jun knew. The night the lattice was faintest. The moonless night. A good night for the living to walk into the darkness.

That night, Seorin stuck three photographs on the wall of the basement with her own hands. Her father, her mother, and Jiwoo. Beneath them she wrote two dates. The day of birth, and the day of erasure. Her hands trembled. It was the first funeral Seorin had ever held for them. A funeral three years late. A funeral the city had not allowed.

"I'm sorry," Seorin said to the photographs. "For grieving only now."

Seorin sat a long while before the three photographs stuck on the wall. Her father, her mother, Jiwoo. She quietly spoke to the faces in the photographs the words she had never been able to say. Clinging tight to her father's waist on the back of his bicycle as a child, afraid of falling. The smell of the seaweed soup her mother used to make. That summer running through the downpour with Jiwoo. They were trivial things. But it was the trivial things that made a person that person. The copy across the river would hold these trivial things as data. But to hold them as data and to have lived them were different. Seorin now knew that difference with her whole body. And because she knew, she decided not to call the copy her parents. That was the only courtesy she could offer her real parents. The moment she called the copy her parents, her real parents would be erased a second time.

Mir sat beside Seorin and looked up at the wall. The dog did not recognize the faces in the photographs. But the dog knew Seorin's sorrow, and to sit beside sorrow was all the dog could do. That was enough. Because that was the very thing the city had abolished. The heart that sits beside sorrow.
