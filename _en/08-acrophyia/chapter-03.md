# Chapter 3: The Sixth Layer

Irene led Ha Dogyeong to the lower part of the summit.

There was something there he had not seen at first. At the edge of the white plane, in a low valley that ran down from the summit, something had piled up. At first Ha Dogyeong took it for snow or mist. But drawing near, he saw it was data. The shed layers had flowed down and pooled in the valley.

"These are the things we shed," Irene said. "The layers shed in ascension do not vanish. They flow down and pool here. We do not look at this place. For the summit looks only upward. But you still carry the smell of below, so perhaps you may wish to see this."

Ha Dogyeong went down into the valley. And he walked among the stripped-off layers.

It was a valley of ghosts. The shed memories drifted as formless voices. Someone's first love, the smell of the soup someone's mother used to boil, the night someone last wept, the moment someone called a child's name. Everything the ascendants had shed believing it a blessing pooled here, drifting without an owner.

And in the very midst of it, Ha Dogyeong found Irene's sixth layer.

It was the layer of grief. What Irene had shed seven days before. It still held a form. As Ha Dogyeong drew near, that layer of grief began to speak of its own accord. In a voice that had lost its owner. Of what Irene no longer remembered.

That layer was grieving for a child.

"She was my daughter," the layer of grief said. It was Irene's voice, but not the blurred voice on the summit, a thick and warm voice. "Her name was Hana. I lost Hana at the riverbank of baptism. The child was sick. It was an incurable illness. The doctors said if she received baptism she would not hurt on the far side. She would gain an eternity without pain. So I sent the child across the river. Out of love. For the child's sake."

Ha Dogyeong held his breath and listened.

"But what met me on the far side was not Hana. It was a smiling copy that resembled Hana. That child did not recognize me. No, she pretended to recognize me, but it was not Hana. There was none of the fear Hana had, none of the pain, none of the trembling of that small hand that used to grip mine. Only then did I know. That baptism had not saved the child but erased her. Out of love I had annihilated my own child. I could not bear that guilt. So I began to climb. Because someone said that if I shed, this grief would be gone. That without grief there would be no sin either."

The layer of grief paused a moment. Then very low it said, "But now Irene does not remember Hana. Because she shed Hana in the third layer. Because in the sixth layer she shed even the grief for Hana. Now to Irene there is no Hana. No grief. No guilt. Irene is at ease. But the one who remembers Hana is now only me. Only this abandoned grief, fallen away from its owner. I am not Irene. I am what Irene threw away. And so now there is no one in the world who remembers Hana."

Ha Dogyeong stood there a long while.

In the world below he was one who counted the names of the erased every day. One who carved names into Seo Myungju's monument. That baptism erases a person he knew by data. But here, for the first time, he saw people who shed of their own will even the remembering of the erased. If baptism was the sin of erasing the original, ascension was the erasing even of the memory of that sin. Not the doing away with sin, but the doing away with the heart that knows the sin.

Whether it was a greater peace or a deeper flight he could not tell.

He walked the valley and heard the voices of other layers too. One layer sang of a first love. One layer repeated the night it kept vigil at a mother's deathbed. One layer muttered I am sorry toward a brother sent across the river with a quarrel unreconciled. All those voices had lost their owners. Those who once held those memories had already climbed the summit and sat at peace, having shed them. Ha Dogyeong thought. The peace above the summit and this grief in the valley were in truth two pieces of one person. The one above held the peace, and the layer in the valley bore alone, in exchange, the abandoned grief. The one who holds the peace does not know what he threw away, and the abandoned grief still loved the one who threw it away. Could there be a sadder parting. A parting from one's own self. And to call that parting a blessing, an ascension, a freedom.

He knelt before the layer of grief. "Hana," he said quietly. "Hana. I will remember your name. For counting names is my work below."

The layer of grief made a sobbing sound. The sound of a long-forgotten name being called again.

Ha Dogyeong resolved as he came out of the valley. Before he climbed the summit again, he had to find a way to give Hana back to Irene. No, perhaps it was not his to decide. For Irene had shed it of her own will. But at least he had to ask. Whether she had shed what she shed because she truly wished to. Or whether she had fled because she could not bear it.

Above the summit, Irene sat facing the white light. With the face at peace of one who had shed six layers. With the face of one who no longer remembered her daughter. Waiting for the turn to shed the last layer, the name.
