# Chapter 6: The One Who Comes Down

The road down into the valley was far harder than the road up the summit.

Climbing, the burdens fell away one by one and the body grew light. But going down it was the reverse. The shed layers clung back on one at a time, and each time Irene sank down in a pain like flesh growing back. The fifth layer, joy, returned. The fourth layer, regret, returned. When regret returned she wore, for the first time, a face that looked ready to weep. But grief had not yet returned, so she could not weep.

Ha Dogyeong stayed at her side. He recalled Seo Myungju's words again. The higher you climb, the more you will forget the road down. He had not forgotten. On the summit that strips away all else, he had held to the one road down. It had led him back below, and now Irene too was coming down along that road.

At last the two reached the valley.

The layer of grief was there. Irene's sixth layer, shed seven days before. The one that, having lost its owner, called a single name alone. Irene stood before it. And gazed at it a long while.

"Is that me?" she asked.

"It is the you that you shed," Ha Dogyeong said. "Embrace it again and Hana returns. Grief returns. Guilt returns. The fact that you are a mother who erased her daughter all returns. You grow heavy again. So heavy you can never climb the summit again."

Irene reached out her hand. And stopped. That hand trembled.

"I am afraid," she said. "If I embrace it I will hurt again. The loss of Hana will grow vivid again. That out of love I annihilated my own child, I will have to know it and live with it every day again. On the summit I did not have to know."

"I understand," Ha Dogyeong said quietly. "That is why this is yours to decide. I neither stop nor urge you. Because that is what we learned in the world below. Crossing the river, not crossing it, coming down against it, all of it one must decide for oneself. Not even a God decides in one's place. Only, decide with the eyes open."

Irene looked at Ha Dogyeong. And once she looked up at the summit. Up there, the white peak. True Nirvana with nothing at all. A place reached by shedding only the last layer. A place that does not hurt. A place that is nothing at all.

And she embraced the layer of grief.

In that instant, the grief that had long drifted alone in the valley poured into her. Irene knelt and wailed. Hana. Hana. My daughter. I sent you across the river. Out of love. Not knowing it would erase you. She wept a long while. The weeping she had not once been able to weep until she had shed six layers, the valley received it all. It was a harrowing grief, and at the same time a grief in which she became a whole person again. The grief of a hurting mother returning, a mother better than being no one at all.

When the weeping subsided, Irene's face was no longer blurred. The features were distinct. Heavy with grief, and because of that weight, all the more the face of a person.

"Where do we go now?" she asked in a wet voice.

"Below," Ha Dogyeong said. "We climb down against the river Noa opened. It is not a complete return. Hana does not come back. What was erased stays erased. But you can return to this side in a body full of flaws, as one who remembers Hana. Below, two riverbanks are open. The riverbank of baptism and the riverbank of the covenant. And now a third road will come to be too. The road down from the summit. The road of those who take back what they shed and choose to remember."

The moment Irene embraced the layer of grief and became a whole person again, Ha Dogyeong would remember a long time. It resembled the moment in the world below when Noa climbed back against the river and wept. Noa regained on this side the tears he lost on the far side, and Irene regained in the valley the grief she shed on the summit. The direction differed, but it was the same thing. To take back, of one's own will, what one had erased. To shoulder again, in order to be human, what one had shed to grow at ease. Ha Dogyeong thought. Perhaps to become human is not to gain something, but to choose to shoulder again the weight one had lost. A God was light. Because it had no weight. A person was heavy. Because a person walked bearing the whole weight of loving and losing and grieving and regretting. And that weight was another name for being alive.

The two left the valley. Behind them, a few ascendants who had come down from the summit searched the valley for the layers they had shed. Some found their layer and embraced it, some in the end could not find it and wept. Some came as far as the valley and returned to the summit after all. For that too was a choice. No one stopped them.

On the road down Ha Dogyeong told Irene the story of the world below. Of the two riverbanks. Of the people deciding their river with their eyes open. Of the dawn on which one could walk to the riverbank of baptism, to the riverbank of the covenant, or by the road of leaving the city without crossing any river. And of an old scientist named Seo Myungju. One who counts the names of the erased. Irene listened a long while, then asked, "Why does she count? Counting does not bring the dead back." Ha Dogyeong answered, "She counts not to bring them back. She counts so they are not forgotten. If there is even one who counts, that person is not wholly gone. Just as you chose to remember Hana." Irene was silent a long while at that. Then she said low, "Then I will be one who counts too. Hana. And the names of the other layers I met in the valley. The layer that sang of a first love, the layer that kept vigil at a mother's deathbed, the layer that said it was sorry to a brother. I will count all those names too. Because if no one counts them, they are alone in the valley forever." Ha Dogyeong nodded. One who came down from the summit had become one who counts. With that, this journey was not in vain.

Above the summit the ascendants still sat. Those who had shed all their layers, with nothing even left to be shaken. They sat facing the white light of the peak, waiting for the turn to shed the last layer. The doctrine of Acrophyia did not fall. Only, even in that highest place, faces turned toward below had for the first time come to be.

Ha Dogyeong and Irene climbed down against the river that flows back. With each layer of the palace they passed, the burden grew, and the more the burden grew, the more human they became. At last, far below, the two riverbanks of Gangnam came into view. The place where the white light of baptism and the blue light of the covenant flow side by side. The place where Seo Myungju's monument stands beside them. The place where the names of the erased are carved each day.

Ha Dogyeong thought. He would carve one new name into the monument. Hana. A child erased at the riverbank of baptism. A name forgotten by the world, drifting alone in the valley. Now that a mother who remembers that name had returned, that name would never be forgotten again.

Acrophyia ends here.

The summit did not fall. The ones who shed still shed, and the doctrine of ascension still shone with white light in the highest place. Whether Acrophyia was right or wrong no one knew. Whether erasing oneself is freedom or flight, whether one who has shed all memory is a Buddha or a snuffed-out lamp, not even the God could answer. Only one thing had changed. That even at the highest layer, there was now a road going down. That one could choose to take back what one shed. That one could choose, with the eyes open, to grow heavy.

In that paradise where baptism erased the original and ascension erased even the copy, for the first time someone chose to grow heavy again of their own will. That person, with the face of a hurting mother, carrying the name of an erased child, went down quietly toward the two riverbanks.

No one blamed the summit. Only this time, even in the highest place, the eyes that look below grew one by one.
