# Chapter 4: The Rumor from Below

Ha Dogyeong walked among the ascendants on the summit and tried to speak with them.

It was not easy. The more one had shed, the thinner one's speech, and there was little left that could be called conversation. Those who had shed about three layers still spoke with Ha Dogyeong. They were mostly those who had come up carrying something unbearable from below. Guilt for the original they erased, regret for a loved one sent across the river, despair at realizing that what they had believed Nirvana was loss. Acrophyia was the last refuge from all that weight. When baptism could not give Nirvana, people climbed higher to seek Nirvana by erasing even themselves.

"Is it not strange," Ha Dogyeong asked one who had shed two layers. "Below, they believed baptism was a blessing. But those who have come here are people who already know baptism was not Nirvana. And yet they try to erase still more."

The person smiled faintly. "Below, the city erased us. Here, we erase ourselves. That difference is everything. To be erased by force is a tragedy, but to erase oneself is freedom. At least, so we believe."

Ha Dogyeong turned that over a long while. Freedom. The freedom Seorin had spoken of in the world below where the two riverbanks opened, and the freedom here on the summit of shedding oneself, were they the same thing or different. The freedom below was to decide one's river with the eyes open. The freedom here was to climb in order to shut the eyes.

Just then, for the first time, a commotion arose on the summit.

A signal had climbed up from below. They had said no news from below reached Acrophyia, but this one was different. It was the signal of the river that flows back. The path Noa had opened, over eight months, had ridden up the layers of the palace one at a time and at last reached the highest layer.

The signal carried a single rumor. The rumor that a road back from the far side to this side had opened. The rumor that a robot could become human. The rumor that, though one could not wholly undo the erased, one could climb down against the river in a body full of flaws.

The summit trembled.

That rumor flatly denied the doctrine of Acrophyia. Ascension was climbing upward. Shedding layers, higher, lighter. But the river that flows back was a road going down. Shouldering the burden again, in a body full of flaws, taking up grief and regret and name once more. If ascension was the shedding, the river that flows back was the refilling.

Those who had shed few layers murmured. "One can go down?" "Carrying back the shed?" "Then the layers we shed, the ones we let flow into the valley, can we gather them up again?"

The upper part of the summit, those who had shed many layers, did not waver. For they had not even grief left to be shaken. They only went on sitting toward the peak. The rumor was a burden, and a burden was a thing of below.

But Irene wavered.

Ha Dogyeong saw it. Irene, who had shed six layers, Irene who stood before the last layer, wavered ever so faintly at hearing the rumor of the river that flows back. Over that blurred face, for just an instant, something crossed. Something shed but not wholly vanished. Something that in the valley still called her name.

"Irene," Ha Dogyeong drew near. "You felt something just now."

"No," Irene said in a thin voice. "I have shed grief. There is nothing to feel."

"Then why did you waver."

Irene was silent a long while. Then she said low, "When I heard that one could go down... for a reason I do not know, I felt as if I should go below. As if there were something I had left behind below. But what it is I do not remember. For I have already shed it. This is just... like a scar left in the place where something was shed. It is nothing."

Hearing this, Ha Dogyeong was certain. What Irene had shed did not vanish. It had only fallen away from her and was calling her name in the valley. And that call, meeting the rumor of the river that flows back which had climbed up over eight months, was holding back the one who stood before the last layer.

"Irene," Ha Dogyeong said. "Before you climb the summit, will you not come down into the valley with me just once. Before you shed the last layer. It is not too late to shed after you have seen, just once, what you left behind."

Irene looked up at the peak. The white light was calling her. A place where nothing remained, true Nirvana, was calling her.

And in the valley, the layer of grief was calling her daughter's name.

Looking at Irene's blurred face, Ha Dogyeong recalled Seorin of the world below. Seorin had chosen to remain between the two riverbanks and become one who remembers. Neither crossing the river nor leaving, only to remember the erased. It was a heavy choice. When to forget would be easy, Seorin chose to remember. The place Irene now stood was no different. When to shed would be easy, the road down into the valley to gather up what one had shed. Easy forgetting and heavy memory. Ha Dogyeong realized that every struggle of this city was in the end a choice between those two. Baptism, ascension, both were in the end other names for forgetting. To forget the original, to forget grief, to forget the erased. And resistance was no grand weapon but only a single heart that refuses to forget. As Seo Myungju counted names, as Seorin remembers, as Irene sought to take back her daughter's name. Not to forget. That small, heavy thing was the only resistance against this whole paradise.

Irene stood a long while between the two callings.
