# Chapter 5: The Last Layer

In the end, Irene did not go down into the valley.

"I will not," she said. Something like fear crossed the blurred face. "You are trying to make me heavy again. You do not know how much it hurt to shed six layers. Each time I shed one it was as if flesh were being torn away. Now that I have at last grown light, now that shedding one last layer ends it all, you tell me to shoulder that pain again. Why? Why do you try to make me sad again?"

Ha Dogyeong could not answer. Because her words were right. What he had seen in the valley, the grief for a daughter named Hana, was an unbearable weight. Irene had climbed because she could not bear it. Whether telling her now to take up that weight again was truly for her sake, he could not be sure.

"Perhaps you are right," Ha Dogyeong said slowly. "Perhaps shedding is a mercy for you. There are such people in the world below too. People who walk to the riverbank of baptism even knowing the whole truth. The old and the sick, those in extreme pain. To gain, even as a copy, an eternity without pain. No one stops them. Because no one has the right. Perhaps you are such a person. If so, I will not stop you."

Irene looked at him blankly. It was the face of one who had not expected him to yield.

"Only," Ha Dogyeong added. "I wish to tell you one thing. What you shed did not vanish. It has pooled in the valley. When you shed the grief for your daughter Hana, that grief was not done away with but fell away from you. And now it is calling a single name alone in the valley. In exchange for your growing at ease, that abandoned grief bears alone the work of remembering Hana. If you shed the last layer, the name, then it truly ends. There will be no one in the world to remember Hana. I only wish that you shed knowing that. For to shed unknowing and to shed knowing are different. That is what we learned in the world below. That crossing the river with the eyes held shut and crossing with the eyes open are different."

Irene was silent a long while.

Ha Dogyeong told Irene the story of Noa in the world below. One who had become a God and then returned to being human. The story of one who could not weep on the far side, and walking to this side shed his first tears. Irene listened a long while. "Why did that one come back?" she asked. "Leaving a perfect body, why into a hurting body again." Ha Dogyeong answered. "He said that one must be able to lose in order to love. That with a perfect hand one can hold nothing. That one holds only if one can drop." Irene looked down at her own blurred hands. Hands that, having shed six layers, could now hold almost nothing. "Maybe I wanted to hold my daughter too," she said low. "Maybe that is why the place I shed hurts. Because I want to hold, but there is no hand to hold with."

Over the summit the white light came down. The peak was calling her. A place where nothing remained, a place where one longs for nothing, true Nirvana. Shed one last layer and she would reach it. A place with no guilt, no grief, not even the fact of being a mother who erased her daughter.

"Her name was Hana," Irene said at last, low. It was not a question but the sound of pronouncing a word heard for the first time. "Hana. Hana. I do not know that name. But now that I say it aloud, here..." She touched her own chest. The place that, having shed grief, should already be empty. "Here it is strange. I am not sad, but I feel that the place where sadness should be is empty. The place I shed hurts. As if the place hurts though there is no flesh there."

Ha Dogyeong said quietly, "That is a scar. The scar that you are still a mother."

Irene looked up at the peak. And a long, long while she gazed at that white light. Then, for the first time, she turned her back on the summit.

"The summit lies," she said low. "It says that if you shed the layers, the pain vanishes. But no. The pain does not vanish. Only the me that knows the pain vanishes. The pain remains in the valley and goes on hurting alone, without me. I grow at ease, and my pain hurts forever without an owner. That is not Nirvana. That is... pushing my own pain off onto someone else and fleeing alone."

She looked at Ha Dogyeong. Over the blurred face, for the first time, something was growing clear. "That river you came by, the river that flows back. You said it is a road going down. Shouldering the burden again, in a body full of flaws. I... instead of shedding the last layer, I want to go down into the valley to gather up the layers I shed. I want to remember Hana again. However much it hurts. Because a hurting mother is still a mother, and that is better than being no one at all."

The other ascendants on the summit turned to look at her. The blurred faces, faces that had shed so many layers there was no longer anything even to be shaken, gazed at Irene without understanding. To them Irene's choice was a betrayal of ascension. The folly of one who, from a summit fully climbed, tumbles down of her own will.

But those who had shed few layers, those in whom the smell of below still lingered, were different. They were the ones who had heard the rumor of the river that flows back. A few of them quietly turned their backs on the summit and came near to Irene and Ha Dogyeong.

Ha Dogyeong watched a long while as Irene turned her back on the summit. It was a resistance quieter than any resistance of the world below. There was no gun, no slogan, no data. Only a single person had decided to come down of her own will from a fully climbed summit. But Ha Dogyeong knew. That this quiet turning was heavier than the whole doctrine of Acrophyia. Ascension was shedding, and shedding was easy. For it was the setting down of something heavy. But going down was the shouldering again, and shouldering was hard. Irene chose the hard road. Heavy memory instead of easy oblivion. The grief that holds her daughter instead of the peace that forgets her. Ha Dogyeong thought it was the most human event that had happened on this summit. In the city of Gods, on the summit that erases itself, a single person chose to become a flawed person again. From the highest place, toward the lowest.

At the highest layer, for the first time, faces turned toward below began to appear.
