# Chapter 2: The Ones Who Shed

Climbing was unlike walking.

With each layer he passed, Ha Dogyeong felt something falling away from him. On the first layer of the palace, the weight of the body vanished. On the second layer, the memory of hunger and thirst grew faint. On the third layer, the sense of time dulled. The road up to Acrophyia was itself a road of shedding. The higher he went, the lighter his burden, and the lighter his burden, the more even the reason he had come up grew dim.

He held to Seo Myungju's last words. Remember the road down. Do not forget the river that flows back. He climbed reciting that sentence like an incantation. On this road that stripped away all else, laboring not to shed that one sentence.

At last he reached the highest layer.

Acrophyia was unlike the palace. If the palace was a city of Gods full of gold and light, Acrophyia was an empty white summit. There was no ornament. No buildings, no streets. Only a broad white plane rose gently upward into a single summit, and upon that summit people sat here and there. All of them looked upward, toward the peak of the summit. No one spoke.

One person came toward Ha Dogyeong. The face was strangely blurred. It had features, but there was a sense of something less than filled in over them. As if the color had been erased little by little from a drawing.

"You have come from below," the person said. Even the voice was somehow thin. "There is a smell. The smell of one still with much burden. The smell of heavy memory."

"Who are you?" Ha Dogyeong asked.

The person hesitated a moment. As if groping for their own name. "Irene. That is what I was called. Once. Now that I have shed six layers, only about half of that name remains."

"Six layers, you say. What have you shed?"

Irene gestured up the summit. "Those who climb here are the ones who have received baptism. Those who have already shed the flesh and crossed to the far side. Below, they believe that is the end. That having become a machine, one has entered Nirvana. But we know. That even after becoming a copy, something still remains. Grief remains. Regret remains. The guilt for the original one erased remains. The face of a loved one remains. So long as such things remain, it is not Nirvana. It is only the old pain held in a painless body."

Ha Dogyeong went cold down the spine. "And so you shed them?"

"One layer at a time." Irene nodded. "We call it ascension. The first layer is possession. Even on the far side we try to hold something. We shed that. The second layer is the future. The wish to become something. We shed that. The third layer is relation. The heart that longs for someone. That too we shed. The fourth layer is regret. The fifth layer is joy. And the sixth layer..." Irene's blurred face grew for a moment more blurred still. "The sixth layer was grief. I am no longer sad now. For I have shed grief."

"Then what remains at the last?"

Irene looked up at the peak of the summit. At that peak there was no one. Only there was white light. "The name. The last layer is the name. Shed the name and ascension is complete. A place where nothing at all remains to be erased. A place where one longs for nothing, regrets nothing, and does not even know who one was. We call it true Nirvana. Baptism below erases the original. Our ascension erases even the copy. Only of one's own will, one layer at a time, believing it a blessing."

Ha Dogyeong gazed at Irene a long while. A person who had shed six layers. A person who had shed even grief. That face was at peace, but how that peace differed from emptiness Ha Dogyeong could not tell.

"Why," he asked carefully, "did you begin to shed? Before you shed the first layer, what was so hard to bear?"

Irene made to answer, then stopped. And was silent a long while. Then, low, in an almost startled voice, she said, "I do not know. That... I think I shed that in the third layer. Why I began to shed was stripped away along with relation. It was because of a certain person, but who that person was I no longer remember."

Ha Dogyeong suddenly recalled Noa. The God who had walked back from the far side to this side and wept. Noa had climbed back against the river to regain the tears, the grief, the being-able-to-die he had lost. But here the people of Acrophyia walked in exactly the opposite direction. The very things Noa sought to regain, they were shedding one layer at a time. Grief, relation, regret. To Noa they were the proof of being human, and to the ascendants they were burdens to be shed. The same thing was a treasure to one and a shackle to another. Ha Dogyeong could not tell which was right. Whether growing heavy like Noa was the road of life, or growing light like the ascendants was salvation. He felt only one thing. Noa gained the face of a person by becoming able to weep, and the ascendants grew more blurred of face the more they shed. That a face grows blurred seemed to him, somehow, no good omen.

Over the summit the white light came down quietly. For the first time Ha Dogyeong felt fear. He knew that baptism erases a person across the river. But here people were erasing themselves. And they called it climbing, blessing, freedom.
