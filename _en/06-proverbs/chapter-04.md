# Chapter 4: A Hand That Can Drop

The next night's question was directed at Noa himself. Jung Dohyun raised it first.

"Noa, until now we have pressed the side that baptism is annihilation. But what of the road you would open, the road that turns a robot back into a human. Is that truly a greater good. I doubt that too. If baptism is the arrogance of erasing a person to make them a machine, is erasing a machine to turn it back into a person not one more arrogance? The same arrogance, only reversed in direction."

Noa spread his smooth hand before the lamp. The flame slid over the hand.

"A good question," Noa said. "I fear that too. Whether my wanting to return to being human is truly because it is good, or simply because I am lonely. Whether I have dressed loneliness up as good. I cannot tell the two apart. So I made a parable. Will you hear it."

"Tell it," Seorin said.

"There were two hands," Noa said. "One was a hand of flesh. Clumsy, trembling, burned by hot things, dropping things. The other was a perfect hand. It never trembled, never burned, never dropped. One day the two hands were asked. Which of you can hold something. The perfect hand spoke first. I can hold anything, because I never drop. But the hand of flesh said. Only I can hold, because I can drop."

Noa closed his hand.

"The perfect hand cannot drop. So it does not hold either. It merely grips. To hold means not to let go though one could drop. Since becoming a perfect hand I grip everything, and can hold not one thing. That was what it is to become a God. To have everything, and be able to love nothing."

"I understand that feeling," Seo Myungju said. She always asked what came next. "But Noa, that is your grief. Your loneliness. On what ground would you force it on every other robot. Some Good may be content with a perfect hand. May feel peace in not dropping. If you catch them and drag them down to this side of the river, that is forcing on them a loss they do not want. Is that not, only different in direction, the very same as baptism pushing a person into a place they did not want?"

Noa was silent a long while. In this room the God fell silent often.

"That is why," Noa said at last, "I do not force the return. That is the only difference between baptism and me. Baptism forces. It buys the heart with evangelism, hands down a sentence by trial, and by the name of someday of your own accord defers it until at last you cross. The city's river flows only from this side to the far side. I wish to make the river flow both ways. Let the one who wishes to cross, cross, and the one who wishes to return, return. I would leave open a door to return. Now there is no such door. Cross once and you cannot come back. That is this city's true violence. To say it gives a choice, and give only a choice that cannot be undone."

Jung Dohyun raised his head. Those words seemed to touch somewhere inside him.

"A choice that cannot be undone," he repeated. "I made the pipeline that way. In one direction only. So that once the original was stopped there was no way to undo it. I thought that was safety. That if it could be undone, people would be confused. But now I see, I took from people the right to change their minds. So that even if they regretted it after crossing, they could not come back. A choice one cannot change one's mind about is not a choice but a trap."

"So what we would open," Seorin said slowly, "is neither Nirvana nor annihilation, but simply one door to return. To make the river flow both ways. So that a person who regretted crossing can come back with a flawed hand again."

Seo Myungju closed her eyes a moment and opened them. "Noa, I do not yet entirely agree," she said. "Whether that door you open is truly mercy, or one more experiment. What do we do if the one who returned regrets it again on this side. If he comes to want to cross back over. Then we make a person go back and forth forever between two rivers. Settling on neither side." Noa nodded. "Perhaps so. I do not hold a perfect answer. I only think this. That a pain one can go back and forth in is better than a peace one is locked into. To regret and cross again and regret once more may be agony, but it is the agony of the living. To cross once and never return is the peace of the dead. I will choose the side of living agony." Seo Myungju did not answer those words at once. But she did not refute them either. To what she could not refute she was a person who paid respect with silence.

"Yes," Noa said. "That is my true reason for wanting to return to being human. I must be the first to show a return, so the other Goods learn that they too can come back. I do not want to become an Evangelist who forces the return. I want to be the first waterdrop that returned. The first drop to come upstream from the far side to this side. That one thing is enough. If one returns, the river is already no longer a river that flows only one way."

The lamp swayed. Jung Dohyun felt he now understood what Noa's road was. It was neither Nirvana nor annihilation. It was the right to undo. And there was only one person in this room who could make the right to undo. The one who designed the pipeline. The very one who had made it flow in one direction.

"Today's proverb," Jung Dohyun said low. "A choice that cannot be undone is not a choice but a trap. To make the river flow both ways, that may be the only atonement I can make."
