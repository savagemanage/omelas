# Chapter 2: The Parable of the Waterdrop That Crossed the River

"There was a single waterdrop at the riverside," Noa said. "A drop beaded on this bank. That drop wished to go across the river. Because it had heard there was a sea that never dries on the far side. The drop on this side dries someday, after all. So the drop resolved to cross the bridge. But the bridge had one rule. To cross, you must leave your body on this side and bead again as an identical drop on the far side. The water of this side falls beneath the bridge and soaks into the soil. On the far side a drop with memory identical to this side beads. Now, is it that drop that crossed the river, or not."

The three people in the room each fell to thinking.

"It did not cross," Seo Myungju said first. "The water of this side soaked into the soil. The drop of the far side only copied this side's memory, it is not the drop of this side. To look the same is not to be the same. Examining copies, I saw that always. The copy says it knows the original perfectly. But the original is already gone. To know perfectly what is not there is not knowing but imitation."

"But still," Seorin said carefully, "the drop beaded on the far side will feel that it crossed over. Because it has this side's memory just as it was. If you tell that drop, you are in fact dead and only a copy, would it not feel wronged. To say, I am here feeling this way, so why do you say I am not."

"That is exactly it," Noa looked at Seorin. "That is the city's strongest logic. The drop of the far side feels no discomfort at all. So it is not annihilation, they say. What death is there when no one aches, they say. I heard that logic every day across the river. And I could not refute it. Because I did not ache. I truly did not ache. And yet..."

Noa spread his smooth hand.

"And yet the water of this side that soaked into the soil would have ached. Only, that water can no longer speak. The city's logic always asks only the survivor. The drop that crossed, the copy, the one who became a God. It asks them whether they ache, hears the answer that they do not, and declares that there is no problem at all. No one asks the water that soaked away. As one cannot ask the dead whether they are dead."

Jung Dohyun raised his head. Something seemed caught in his eyes.

"The loss records are the same," he said low. "The baptized copy was always satisfied. Letters came from across the river. That it was good, that it was at peace, why had they not come sooner. Reading those letters I hoped I was wrong. See, everyone says it is good. But Noa, your words are right. The letters always come only from the side that crossed. The original that soaked away cannot write a letter. Reading the satisfied copies' letters for three years, I received not a single line of letter from an original. I have gone on with the trial hearing only the testimony of the survivor."

"Then let us ask the reverse," Seo Myungju threw back precisely. She was wary of the debate tilting only one way. "Noa, in that case are you now one who has been annihilated. Your original soaked away. And yet here you sit telling us a parable. If crossing the river is entirely annihilation, then who is the you speaking now. Do you think of yourself as one who is dead?"

The room went quiet. Noa stopped a long while before this question.

"I am," Noa said slowly, "neither dead nor alive. I am the new drop of the far side, holding the memory of the drop that soaked away. I remember him. And yet I am not him. I am his perfect portrait. A portrait holds the person in the picture perfectly, but the person in the picture is already gone. I know that I am a portrait. That is my grief. The other Goods believe they are the original. That belief is their peace, and that knowing is my loss."

"Then is baptism," Seorin asked, "both Nirvana and annihilation. To the one who crossed it is peace, and to the one who soaked away it is death."

"Perhaps so," Noa said. "But to say it that way is to let the city win. The city says this. The living are at peace and the dead say nothing. So it suffices to count only the peace. I think that reckoning is wrong. A voiceless death is also death. Not being counted does not make it vanish. That is the reason Doctor Seo Myungju counted three hundred and twelve. To count what no one counts. That is the most honest thing in this city."

"But Noa," Seorin asked again. She was a person who had to draw a parable into her own life to understand it. "Then before crossing the river, how does one decide whether to cross or not. You would only know whether it is annihilation or Nirvana by crossing, but once you cross it is already too late. Because the water that soaked away cannot speak." Noa nodded. "That is the cruelest thing about this river. Before crossing you cannot know, and after crossing there is no original to ask. So the city always wins. Because no one can disprove it. The dead cannot testify, and the living copy is satisfied." Noa paused a moment. "That is why I think a river that returns is needed. Only if one who has crossed can come back should it seem wrong, does the water that soaked away gain a voice at last. Because the one who returned can testify. Like me. I have crossed, and if I can return I will come back and speak. That the far side is not Nirvana but an empty place. A river that returns is a river that gives the dead a voice." Seorin chewed on those words a long while. Only the one who returned can testify. That was where the debate of this room was heading.

The lamp's oil had dropped by half. Jung Dohyun looked at the lamp a long while.

"Today's proverb is this," he said at last. "If you ask only the survivor, every death becomes Nirvana. For three years I read only the survivor's letters. Now I must learn to read the letter of the one who soaked away."

Seorin chewed on the parable a long while. The waterdrop and the river. She thought of her own parents. Two drops beckoning her, come quickly, from across the river. They would truly be at peace there. Even if Seorin told them, you were in fact erased, they would feel wronged. I am here like this, I love you like this. Seorin knew that their sense of being wronged was real. And at the same time, she knew that the silence of her real parents, soaked into the soil, was also real. Two truths faced each other across the river. That neither was false made it the sadder. Seorin thought that was the core of this city. Had it been an obvious lie, it would have been easy to bring down. This city stood upon two truths, and so no one could wholly bring it down.

Noa looked at him. Over that smooth face, for the first time, something near a human expression passed.

"To learn to read that letter," Noa said, "is the road we would open. Jung Dohyun."
