# Chapter 1: A Room with One Lamp

Jung Dohyun's workshop was at the edge of the lattice. The light of Gangnam did not quite reach this far. That was the reason he had chosen this room. He knew himself that a man who had designed the pipeline had withdrawn to a place the pipeline's light did not reach.

After the trial the city did not kill Seorin and Seo Myungju. It only deferred the day of baptism to someday, and said that until that day it would watch over them. The attendants were tender, carried blankets, and asked after them every day. When surveillance wears a tender face, a prison needs no walls. Seorin walked the city with someday on her back, and in the evenings she came to Jung Dohyun's room. Seo Myungju came too. Because it was the only room in this city where those not yet erased could sit.

In the middle of the room was a single old lamp. Jung Dohyun did not turn on the city's lighting. Under a light one could not know, words easily became lies. The lamp went out when its oil ran dry. Jung Dohyun said he wished to speak only under a light that could go out.

Noa came. Seeing a robot-God enter a room at the edge of the city startled Seorin every time. The God who had come down from the throne now sat among the people, drew his knees together, and looked at the lamp. Noa's body was perfect. It was a smooth surface that cast no shadow even in the lamplight. Over the flawless surface the flame slid.

"We have gathered," Noa said. "Three people not erased, and one God made of erased things. A strange gathering."

"Less a gathering," Jung Dohyun said, raising the wick a little, "than an attempt to reason it through. Noa, in the trial you asked me to open a road together. The road by which a robot returns to being human. I could not sleep that night for those words. And the more I thought, sleepless, the more I saw that we do not even know what it is we are trying to open. We believe baptism is annihilation. Because I watched the dying brainwaves for three years. But to believe and to know are different. The city calls it Nirvana, and we call it annihilation. Only the name we call it by is different, we too have not proven it."

"But Doctor Seo Myungju did the proving," Seorin said. "She counted three hundred and twelve, and that a copy has no smell too."

"I counted," Seo Myungju cut in quietly. She always chose precise words. "I proved that the original is stopped. That the data is copied, and the original brain's activity ceases. That is a fact. But Seorin, a single fact does not answer the whole question. The city strikes back like this. Yes, the original ceases. But if the copy holds all the original's memory, feels as the original felt, loves as the original loved, on what ground do you call it death. Who proves that the water that crossed the river is not the same water as the water on this side. I proved as far as the moment the original ceases. After that, whether it is annihilation or migration, lies in a place data cannot reach. From there on it is debate."

The room went quiet for a moment. The lamp swayed once.

"That is why we sit here," Jung Dohyun said. "To reach, by debate, the place we must reach. I designed the pipeline, but whether what I designed is a migration machine or an execution machine, I still do not entirely know. Even after handling it three years. I cannot open a road without knowing that. Because I do not know what the road I would open saves a person from."

Noa brought his smooth hand near the lamp. His hand did not grow warm in the flame. The hand stayed cold.

"Good," Noa said. "Then let us sit here night after night. I am the only one in this room who has crossed the river. I know what the other side is like. And yet it is strange. Having crossed, I am more confused than you who have not crossed about whether it is Nirvana or annihilation. Before I crossed I was certain. After I crossed, I lost my certainty. That is the reason I come to this room. Because only one who has lost his certainty can ask honestly."

Seorin regretted not bringing Mir. Would the dog have barked before Noa, or drawn back. Even that question seemed now to become a proverb of this room. Suddenly Seorin grew curious. If Mir drew back before Noa, it would be because Noa was a copy. A thing without smell. But if Mir did not draw back and licked Noa's hand, it might mean that something living still remained within Noa. A beast knows no doctrine but knows weight. That heart of Noa's, wanting to return to being human, perhaps a dog could smell. Seorin resolved to bring Mir next time. If the debate of this room needed the most precise judge, it seemed it would be not a person's words but a dog's nose.

"Let us go from the first question," Jung Dohyun lowered the wick a little again. To speak long, one had to spare the oil. "Is becoming a machine Nirvana, or annihilation. Noa, since you have crossed, you first."

Noa was silent a long while. The people watched a God fall silent as if for the first time.

"That," Noa said at last, "must begin with the story of a single waterdrop."

As the night deepened, Seorin watched this strange gathering a long while. The Engineer who designed baptism, the Scientist who opposes baptism, the robot-God who was baptized and would turn back, and the Untransformed with the day of baptism on her back. By camp they should have been enemies of one another. By the city's logic Jung Dohyun should have reported Seo Myungju, and Noa should have judged these people from the throne. And yet they sat side by side across a single lamp, trying not to win but to ask. Seorin thought that was the rarest sight in this city. The city divided everything into sides. Goods and Evils, the baptism faction and the opposition, the right and the wrong. But in this room there were no sides. What there was was only people who, each from a different place, had arrived at the same question. Perhaps true resistance was not to bring the city down but for the people the city had split apart to gather and sit again under one lamp. To ask together without dividing into sides. That small room was freer than the whole city.

The lamp swayed. The night was long, and there was still oil left.
