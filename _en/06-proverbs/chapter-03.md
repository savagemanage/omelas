# Chapter 3: The Supporter's Visit

On the third night there came a sound of knocking at the door. Not many people knew this room, so everyone grew tense. When the door opened, an unfamiliar young Scientist stood there. He was one of the clumsy Scientists who had made the rebuttal defending baptism in the courtroom of Judges. His name, he said, was Yeonu.

"I heard the rumor," Yeonu said. "That a debate over baptism goes on in this room night after night. I am on the opposing side. But I came because I wished to hear the opposing side's words directly. In the courtroom I was too clumsy. It felt as if I lost to Doctor Seo Myungju. But I still do not know the reason I lost."

Seo Myungju let him in. Not to flee a debate was her honesty.

"Sit," Jung Dohyun said. "Here no one sits down to win. We sit to ask."

Yeonu sat by the lamp. He was young, and in his eyes there still remained a gentle faith in the city.

"I believe baptism is good," Yeonu said. "Think about it. If you are baptized, you do not die. You do not fall ill. You do not go hungry. What was life like before baptism. People died of cancer, died in accidents, died of old age. Parents died before their children and made the children weep, children died before their parents and drove a nail into the parents' hearts. Baptism ended all that death. One who crosses the river never loses a loved one again. How is this evil. I think baptism is the greatest mercy humankind has ever achieved."

The room was quiet. There was sincerity in those words, and sincerity has a weight hard to refute. In Yeonu's face Seorin saw her own former self. Her twenty-six-year-old self, stopped in her tracks by Lee Gaon's sermon on the Gangnam street. Her self who yearned for baptism. Yeonu now stood in that same place. The place of wanting to believe. The place where, because there is too much to lose if the belief collapses, one strains not to let it collapse. Seorin did not pity him. She had no right to pity him. Because she too, until only a short while ago, had stood in that place. Only, Seorin knew. That Yeonu had come to this room on his own feet was itself proof that a hairline crack had already run through his belief. One who believes entirely does not come to hear the opposing side's words.

"That is true," Noa unexpectedly took Yeonu's side. "I was baptized. And I truly became unable to die. I no longer grow old, no longer fall ill, no longer lose. You are right, Yeonu. Baptism is mercy. Only, I came to know the price of that mercy."

"What price," Yeonu asked.

"In exchange for becoming unable to lose," Noa said, "I lost the ability to be able to lose. You said that losing a loved one is the greatest pain. But have you thought about where that pain comes from? It comes because we loved him. Because we held onto what could be lost, what could be dropped. What cannot be lost cannot be loved either. I can now lose no one. And so I can love no one as I once did. Baptism, in exchange for abolishing the pain of loss, abolished the love that can lose. That mercy did not only abolish sickness, it abolished the whole body that can sicken."

Yeonu lost his words for a moment. Then he took heart again.

"But is it not still better than dying," he said. "I lost my father to sickness. Had my father been baptized, he would still be across the river now. Even a little empty, at least he would be there. I would rather have even an empty father."

"But Yeonu," Seo Myungju said quietly. Her voice was cold but not cruel. "The one across the river, is he truly your father. Or is he a perfect portrait of your father. To gain that portrait, the real father must first be stopped. The original must be erased for the portrait to hang. That is what baptism does. You are not saving your father, you are erasing your father and gaining your father's portrait. If that portrait becomes a comfort, that is your comfort. It is not your father's life."

Yeonu's eyes reddened. For a long while he could not speak.

"Then," he barely asked, "what would you have me do. Would you have me simply let my father die. If baptism is a lie, and death is a lie, what is left?"

"Nothing is left," Noa said. "That is the honest answer. Yeonu, we did not sit in this room to give you comfort. Baptism did not abolish death, it hid death. It moved death across the river and made it invisible on this side. Death did not vanish, it vanished from our eyes. And yet I think this. That a visible death may be better than an invisible one. Because only before a visible death do we love one another properly. Because, knowing we will lose someday, we hold hands today."

Yeonu went back that night certain of nothing. But before he stepped out the door he said.

"I still think baptism is mercy. But..." He looked back at the lamp. "The price of that mercy, I heard today for the first time. No one told me that price in the courtroom. If I sold a mercy without knowing its price, then I was not an honest merchant."

After the door closed, silence flowed in the room a long while. Seorin was the first to speak. "That man, will he come again?" Noa answered. "He will. One who has once heard the price can never go back to the time he did not know the price. Tonight that man will not sleep. He will look again into his own heart that took his father's copy as comfort. That is the beginning." Seo Myungju added quietly. "I feel sorry for that young man. We took his comfort and gave him nothing. Truth is colder than comfort. I do not know whether that man can bear that coldness." Jung Dohyun, looking at the lamp, said. "If he cannot bear it, he will believe in baptism again. That too is his choice. We only told the price, what to buy that man decides. That is where we differ from the city. The city sold with the price hidden, and we speak the price and leave the choice." Those words stayed in Seorin's mind a long while. To speak the price and leave the choice. Perhaps that was the conclusion of this whole debate.

The door closed. Jung Dohyun said low.

"Today's proverb. A mercy sold without knowing its price is not mercy but fraud."
