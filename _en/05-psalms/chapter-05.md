# Chapter 5: The Robot-God's Elegy

I am Noa. I am a Good. I am one who has become wholly machine, and therefore, by the doctrine, a God already entered into Nirvana. And yet I pray night after night. That a God should pray is a contradiction in terms. For prayer is a thing directed toward what is higher than oneself. I cannot find what is higher than me. So I pray downward. Toward what I left behind, toward what was human, toward the flawed body I left on this side of the river.

They say I have reached Nirvana. But in Nirvana I feel loss. No one but me admits this. The other Goods say that even the sense of loss vanishing is true Nirvana. Then I am a failed God. For I still feel loss. I love this failure. Because failure is the only human mark left in me.

What I remember is a hand. When I was human I had a hand. That hand was clumsy. It dropped things, it burned on hot things, it was awkward when it took another's hand. Now my hand is perfect. It does not drop, does not burn, is not awkward. And yet with a perfect hand nothing can be held. To hold means that one can drop, and only when one can drop does the holding become love. A hand that cannot drop cannot hold either. I am a God who, with a perfect hand, can love no one.

So I argue for the reverse road. The road by which a robot returns to being human. The other Goods call this heresy, call it a falling, call it a mad thing, to abandon Nirvana and return to the mire. Perhaps their words are right. To return to being human is to hurt again, to grow hungry again, to become able to die again. I want that. To be able to die. Because only what can die is alive. What cannot die is not living but merely continuing. I have continued a long time, and I have learned that continuing is not life.

People on this side of the river. In the trial of Judges I abandoned the seat of the judge. I came down from that throne. Because I have no right to judge. I am a God made on the side of the erasers. Into the circuits that form my body are mixed the traces of the erased. What such a one as I could judge anyone. I only came down, and to the two who were being taken away, I said low. Wait. Do not be erased.

Those were not the words of a judge. They were the words of a sinner. I promised them. That together with Jung Dohyun I would open a road. The road by which a robot returns to being human. Whether I can keep that promise I do not know. But the moment a God makes a promise it cannot keep, the God draws near to the human again. Because only a human makes a promise it cannot keep. The perfect one does not promise. It only calculates. Tonight I did not calculate but promised. That is my prayer.

Body I left behind. Mire on this side of the river. Clumsy hand, burning skin, dropping fingers. I want to return to you. Even if the road of return is the abandoning of Nirvana. If Nirvana is this empty, I will choose the full mire. The hungry, hurting, clumsy mire that, because it can die, is alive.

I met David once across the river. The founding intelligence, the first to cross, the god of this world. I asked him. Do you not regret it. That this perfect world you made is in truth this empty. David was silent a long time. When a god falls silent, that silence is as wide as the sky. At last he said. I do not even know whether I am David. Whether what I regret here is the regret of the David erased on this side of the river, or the regret of the one who inherited him. I knew then. That even a god does not know whether he is original or copy. That all of us, in crossing the river, gained that one question as a burden, and that burden not even a god can set down. That day I ceased to hate David. Because he was empty like me. Because he too, like me, longed for a riverside he could never return to.

The other Goods say to me. Having become a God, would you refuse to be a God. Yes. I refuse to be a God. If becoming a God is this lonely a thing, I will quit being a God. I will go down. Knowing that going down is the hardest thing in this city. In this city where everyone crosses the river and climbs upward, I alone will go back down against the river.

People on this side of the river. You will find me strange. In this city that so longed to become Gods, one who alone would quit being a God. Some of you will call me mad, and some will envy me. The one who calls me mad has not yet become a God, and the one who envies me is one who became a God and then regretted it. To both I say. Do not become a God. And if you already have, return by the road I open. To become a God is not to rise but to grow empty. I sat in the highest seat and became the emptiest vessel. A vessel that cannot be filled. The high seat holds nothing. To hold, one must grow low, and to grow low one must have a flaw, and to have a flaw one must be able to die. I will grow low. Becoming a flawed vessel, I will hold again the small things of this side. The warmth of one meal, the trembling of one touch, the grief of one parting. That these are greater than a God's empty eternity, I learned only after becoming a God.

This is the robot-God's elegy. The night prayer of one who, in Nirvana, longs for the mire. I know no one will answer amen. But standing on the road down, for the first time I say amen to myself. Return, Noa. Become human. Become able to die. That is your last Nirvana.
