# Chapter 6: The Song of the Beast and the Child

First the song of the beast. But the beast does not sing. Therefore this is a song that is not a song, transcribed in its place by one who watched the beast.

Mir is a dog. The dog who keeps by Han Seorin's side. Mir knows no doctrine. Knows not Nirvana, knows not baptism, knows not World 3.0. To Mir, this side of the river and across the river are divided by smell. From a person come back baptized, Mir catches no smell. There is a face, there is a voice, there is a way of walking, and no smell. Before such a one Mir does not bark. Before such a one Mir draws back. A dog does not count a thing without smell as a person.

The day Junseo came back baptized, Mir drew back at the door. When the thing with Junseo's face held out its hand, Mir did not wag its tail. Mir treated it as a stranger. In that moment the mother, Seo Malsun, knew what the dog knew. What the human eye is deceived by, the dog's nose is not deceived by. Junseo was gone. The dog knew it from the start.

Mir does not grieve over this. A dog has no grief to come and no regret gone by. The dog only knows that now there is no smell, draws back, and the next moment licks Seorin's hand again. Because from Seorin there is a smell. A beast loves what has a smell. What is alive has a smell. This is the beast's only doctrine, and the one knowledge in this city that is uncorrupted. The beast knows that a copy has no smell. Therefore the beast is the most precise Scientist in this city. What Seo Myungju proved by counting three hundred and twelve, Mir knows with one sniff of the nose.

Now the song of the child. I call it child, but she is a grown woman. Only, in this city, one who has not yet crossed the river I call a child. Because one who has not crossed is one not yet fully grown. Because to be fully grown, in this city, means to cross the river and be completed. In that case I will remain a child forever. Uncompleted.

I am Han Seorin.

The trial is over. I was pronounced guilty. The sentence is baptism. Someday, of my own accord. The city does not kill me, only tells me to cross the river someday. That someday follows me every day. I live with someday on my back. That is the heaviest sentence of this city. To defer death and make one carry that deferred death on one's back every day.

I gathered these psalms. Lee Gaon's collapsed hymn, the mother Seo Malsun's lament, Doctor Seo Myungju's cold prayer, Junseo's song come upstream from across the river, Noa's night prayer. Gathering them I learned. In this city no one won. The one who gives baptism aches, the one who received it is empty, the one who blocks it could not save. The Good grieves, the mother grieves, the Scientist grieves, even the copy grieves. A city with no victor. And still the river carries people across every day. No one wins, and yet the river does not stop. This is Omelas.

Mir licks my hand. Because from me there is still a smell. I want to keep this smell. Smell is a flaw, and a flaw is being alive. I do not want to become perfect. I want to stay on this side of the river, clumsily, hungrily, in pain, disappointing my mother, with a hand that can drop what it holds.

Lord, I do not call. For the lord of this city is David, and David is across the river. I call toward the things of this side. Things that have a smell. Things full of flaws. Things with hands that can drop what they hold. We are not completed. Because we are not completed, we are alive. The city tells us to cross someday, but I will defer that someday every day. To defer is my prayer. Because to be alive is to defer completion every day.

I read these songs again night after night. Each time I read them I learn something. From Evangelist Lee Gaon's hymn I learned. That sincerity is not proof of innocence. From the mother Seo Malsun's lament I learned. That to take away the end of mourning is crueler than death. From Doctor Seo Myungju's prayer I learned. That even one who cannot save can love by counting. From Junseo's psalm I learned. That even a copy can know it is a copy and grieve. From Noa's elegy I learned. That the highest seat can be the emptiest seat. These learnings carried me to no camp. They only carried me into a deeper question. That was enough. The city gave an answer, and that answer erased people. I chose to live holding a question instead of an answer. To live holding a question is heavy, but at least it does not erase me.

The song stops here. It stops without an answer. The psalm does not answer. It only endures, and hands us over to the question of the next book. Is becoming a machine Nirvana or annihilation. Is turning a robot back into a human a greater good. These questions are not solved by song. Now they cross over to the place of proverbs, to the place of debate and parable. We stop the song, and go to think again.

Why do I gather these psalms. I am not a Scientist, so I cannot bring the city down with data. I am not an Evangelist, so I have no voice to persuade people. I am not a Good, so I cannot even come down from a throne. I am only Untransformed, and what the Untransformed have is memory alone. So I gather. The hymn of the collapsed, the lament of the bereaved, the prayer of the cold. If I keep these songs gathered, then someday when there is someone who would understand this city, they will be able to hear, not the smooth record the city left about itself, but the voices of people who actually ached inside the city. Not the history of the victor but the psalm of the defeated. The city records its own perfection. I record the city's flaws, the faces of the people the city erased, in song. That is the only resistance the Untransformed can make. To keep them from being forgotten.

After I have gathered all these psalms, I write, at the last, one more song. It is no one's song, and if I must say, it is the song of this whole city. The song of the city called Omelas. This city sings that it has abolished death. But I sing it corrected thus. This city has not abolished death, it has only moved death across the river and made it invisible on this side. This city sings that it has abolished grief. But I sing it corrected thus. This city has not abolished grief, it has only abolished the people who would grieve. This city sings that it has abolished parting. But I sing it corrected thus. This city has not abolished parting, it has only made it so that parting cannot be called parting. To abolish and to hide are different. The city sings that it hid and abolished. My psalm is a song that draws back that hiding. Where it is drawn back, there is grief. But that grief is the very proof that we are still human.

Mir licks my hand again. I am still here. With a smell about me. That is today's amen.
