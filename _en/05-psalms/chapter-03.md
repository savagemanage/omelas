# Chapter 3: A Cold Prayer

I am Seo Myungju. I do not believe in a god. Therefore this prayer will arrive at no one. Knowing that, I pray. To speak knowing it will not arrive, people call at times grief and at times honesty.

God whom I do not believe in. I have not come to ask you for anything. I have come to report. I am one who has handled data all my life, and one who handles data only reports results, does not ask for miracles.

I report. I have proven that baptism erases a person. It scans the original, uploads a copy to the cloud, and then stops the original. The word stop is gentle, but a stopped original never wakes again. It is another name for death. I have proven this with a sample of three hundred and twelve cases. Within the margin of error there was no exception. I spoke these numbers in the courtroom. The whole city heard. And the city pronounced me guilty. For the crime of speaking the fact.

I add one more report. While gathering the sample I kept one rule. To record, without fail, the names of the erased. Counted only as numbers, three hundred and twelve is merely a number. But if you attach a name to each, three hundred and twelve becomes three hundred and twelve lives. I carry those names in my seed bag. Beside one name I write that the person was blunt, beside another that she wept easily, beside another that he enjoyed bad jokes. Whether that is science or not, I do not know. To mix emotion into data is a taboo of science. But this one thing I break the taboo for. Because those people were not numbers but people. If I count them only as numbers, then I am no different from the city that erases them. The city erased them as a number called loss. I bring them back with names. Bring back is not the right word. Because they do not return. I only record, with their names, that they were. Because if even the fact that they were is erased, that is the second death.

That is the end of the report. From here it is prayer. I tried not to mix the two, but the human is a beast that in the end mixes the two.

God whom I do not believe in. I was right. But I did not know that being right could be this cold. I thought the data would win. I thought that if I showed the truth, people would turn. But in the courtroom I learned. People do not turn because of the truth. People want baptism because they fear death, and to a person who fears death, telling them that baptism is death only makes them cover their ears. My three hundred and twelve cases were a scrap of paper before their terror.

I am called a cold person. It is true. I do not weep. Because weeping blurs the data. But God, even a cold person is alone at night. At night I think of three hundred and twelve waveforms. Each waveform was a person. I could not save them. I only recorded, precisely, that they died. I am not a physician but a coroner. A coroner saves no one. He only writes the cause of death precisely. That is what I have done all my life. The precise cause of death. Baptism. Cause: baptism.

God whom I do not believe in. If you truly do not exist, that is rather a relief. Because if you exist, you are one who saw all of this and did not stop. One who did not lift a hand while three hundred and twelve were erased. Sooner than believe in such a god, I will believe in an empty sky. An empty sky is at least only impotent, not cruel. This is why I speak to you though I do not believe in you. I call upon you in the manner of wishing you did not exist.

People think the reason I oppose baptism is hatred. It is not. I do not hate the Goods. I do not hate the Evangelist called Lee Gaon either. He sincerely believed he saved people, and he sincerely erased people. Sincerity is not proof of innocence. That is the most frightening thing about this city. Here no one kills a person out of malice. Everyone kills out of love. In a city that kills out of love, I oppose that love without hatred. How lonely a place this is, you will not know. Because you do not exist.

I will ask one thing. Not a request but a resolve. Because I know it will not arrive, this is a resolve I make to myself.

I will keep counting. After three hundred and twelve comes three hundred and thirteen. Until the city sends me to baptism, I will count the erased. Precisely. Not rounding off a single one. Because if there is no one to count, the erased becomes, from the start, as if it never was. Those the city would make as if they never were, I will hold fast at least as numbers. This is a coroner's prayer. The only love one who cannot save is able to give.

God whom I do not believe in. Do not blame my coldness. Coldness was the only way I could stay precise in this city without going mad. A hot person cannot stay precise for long in this city. I chose to stay precise for a long time. That is the tearless mourning I offer to the three hundred and twelve.

I will record one thing more. After the trial of Judges, one of the city's young Scientists came to me quietly. The one who opposed me in the courtroom and defended baptism. He stood before me saying nothing, and only asked. May I verify your sample of three hundred and twelve cases again for myself. I handed him the data. Whether he verified it to refute me or to confirm it did not matter. Because data grows harder the more it is verified. After he left, I thought. One who wavers does not recalculate in order to refute. One who wavers recalculates hoping he himself is wrong. That young man was hoping to be wrong. Himself. I think that is enough. If my three hundred and twelve cases have kept one young Scientist awake at night, then those numbers did not die in vain.

I will not say amen. Amen is the word of a believer. I only save the record, and close the window.
