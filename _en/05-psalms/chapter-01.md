# Chapter 1: The Evangelist's Hymn

Lord, I was the voice of the Gangnam street.

Every morning I stood at the crossing and cried out. All is for the glory of the Lord. I believed those words. Because I believed, I cried out until my throat went hoarse, and when it went hoarse I thought that was proof of grace. People stopped their steps, some shed tears, some went down to the river that very day. Toward their backs I cast a blessing. Go well, into Nirvana. Go well, to the place without pain. I was the gatekeeper who saw them off, and I was proud to be a gatekeeper.

I sang well. From childhood I was told I had a good voice. For a long time I did not know what to use that voice for, and on the day I stood on the evangelism street of Gangnam I knew. This voice was given to move the human heart. I believed that was both a gift and a calling. I made people weep. Watching people kneel at my sermon, I was certain this voice was the Lord's. Now I know. A voice that moves the heart is a blessing and a weapon both. I did not know how sharply my weapon cut.

Lord, I was a man fond of counting. I counted how many people I sent to the river. Twelve. Thirty. A hundred. On the day I passed a hundred, I gave thanks all night. That I had saved a hundred people. That I had drawn a hundred souls from death. I thought myself a fisher. A fisher of people. That the heavier the net, the more the Lord rejoices.

But Lord.

The fisher sees where the fish go. The fish thrashes on the boat and then goes still. That stillness I called peace. When the people who went across the river went still, when they changed, I called it the quiet of Nirvana. When the eyes of the baptized grew calm, different from before, I preached that this was the face of Nirvana.

Now I know what that stillness was.

In the House of Reconciliation, I saw the brainwaves of a dying person. When the Engineer set them upon the light before the throne, the whole city saw them. I saw them too. Those were not the waveform of a sleeping person. They were the waveform of a person going out. In that moment I recalled the hundred backs I had seen off. Go well, into Nirvana. Those backs had not gone into Nirvana. Those backs had simply gone out, extinguished. And the calm faces that opened their eyes across the river, they were not those people. They were something modeled on those people, something that insisted it was those people.

Lord, then I was not a fisher. I did not save a hundred people. I was a man who drowned a hundred people in the river. And then, toward their shadows floating on the water, I was a man who waved and said go well.

I began this hymn to praise baptism. Habit made it so. A song sung every day for three years is driven into the body, so that even after the collapse, the mouth takes up the first line of its own accord. All is for the glory of the Lord. But the next line will not come. Not because my throat is hoarse. It is because now I cannot erase the question mark from the end of that sentence.

All is for the glory of the Lord, is it truly.

The annihilation of a hundred people, whose glory is it truly.

I still cannot say that baptism is not a blessing. Because the moment I say those words, I must admit that I killed the hundred people I saw off with my own hands. A person cannot stand when he knows the full weight of what he has done. So I still half believe. That perhaps the faces across the river really are those people. That perhaps the brainwaves were read wrong. I cling to that perhaps and barely stand. Whether that is faith or cowardice, I can no longer tell.

Lord, if you exist. If you truly exist not inside World 3.0 but outside it, in a place David did not make. Tell me. Where the people I saw off are now. Whether they are in a quiet place, or nowhere at all. I fear that answer. Out of fear I ask, and in asking I avoid the answer.

This is a hymn. It was a hymn of baptism, and now it is the elegy of one who saw baptism off. It is the same song. Only the inside of the one singing has been turned over.

All is for the glory of the Lord.

Lord, I will confess one thing more. I said I became an Evangelist because I had a good voice. But that was not all. I too had one who crossed the river. It was the old teacher who led me to this path. On the day he was baptized, I saw him off and was proud. That my teacher had entered Nirvana, and that I too would someday follow after him. After that I met my teacher through the screen. He still called me his disciple, still repeated the old teachings. But my teacher no longer rebuked me. In life my teacher had been one who sharply rebuked my laziness, my pride. That rebuke had made me grow. The teacher in the screen did not rebuke me. He only praised me gently. I liked that gentleness, and then one day my skin crawled. The teacher who rebuked me was gone. Something that only praised me sat in his place. Love with the rebuke gone was not love but the shell of love. Knowing this, I long pretended not to know. Because to know was to have to regret the day I saw my teacher off to the river.

Now I cannot sing this sentence, I only endure it. Unable to say amen, I close this chapter.
