# Chapter 3: Two Riverbanks

Three days were short and yet long.

Through those three days the city was strangely quiet. The Evangelists crying out baptism grew fewer, and the Untransformed carried off to the house of reconciliation stopped. The whole city held its breath and waited for the two riverbanks the God had promised. In the streets people lowered their voices and asked. On which riverbank will you stand.

On the first day, Han Seorin and Seo Myungju were released from the house of reconciliation. On the attendant's face there was none of that old certainty. "It is the command of the founding intelligence," the attendant said in a trembling voice. "Now you must walk on your own. Whichever way."

The two who had knocked on the wall between them every night stood, for the first time, side by side beneath the same sky. Seo Myungju still had not let go of that bag of seeds.

"Doctor. Have we won?" Seorin asked.

"No," Seo Myungju said. "The city has not lost to us, it has passed the choice on to us. Before, the city dragged people to the riverbank. That was the city's responsibility. Now the city has left the two riverbanks open and withdrawn. This may not be our victory but the way the God shares out his own sin onto the people."

Seorin felt the weight of those words. The city had lost its perfection, but in exchange it was sharing out its sin as well to the people. Before, the city led people across the river, and so the people were at ease. Because they did not have to choose. Because the God said it was right, and they had only to follow. Now that the city has withdrawn, that ease vanishes too. The people must now decide their river for themselves, and if they decide wrong, that wrong is wholly their own. Seorin thought that was the true weight of freedom. Freedom was not obtaining an answer, but shouldering for oneself a question that has no answer. The city had long carried that heavy burden in the people's stead, and in exchange it had stripped the people of their right to choose. Now the burden had returned. Heavy, but a burden that was one's own.

On the second day, Lee Gaon came to Seorin. The collapsed Evangelist did not wear his old white robe. Since that night in Proverbs he had never again cried out baptism in the streets of Gangnam. He had lost his voice.

"My sin was not that I led people to baptism," Lee Gaon said. "My sin was that I stripped people of their choice. I gave them the answer before they had asked for themselves. I thought that was love. But it was crossing a person's river in their place. Now I will not take either side. I will only let people cross the river with their eyes open."

Seorin looked into Lee Gaon's eyes. That clear gaze she had once, before Gangnam Station Exit 10, taken for the eyes of one who had already seen something and come back. Now in those eyes there was a question instead of certainty. Seorin thought that was the more human gaze.

On the third day, the Ones Who Walk Away returned from beyond the lattice. Not dragged in, but walking in of their own accord upon receiving Seo Myungju's signal. Mir was with them. The moment Seorin saw Mir she knelt and buried her face in that white fur. The dog confirmed by instinct that Seorin had not yet been erased, and wagged its tail. Among those who returned was a boy named Jun. The boy who, on the night of Exodus, had asked Seo Myungju why she was leaving.

"Sister. Are we not leaving now?" Jun asked.

"I don't know," Seorin said honestly. "We came to see the two riverbanks. Whether the city truly opens the riverbank of the covenant, or whether this too is one more lie. We see that, and then we decide again. Whether to stay, to leave, or to cross the river."

When night came, two riverbanks appeared in the heart of Gangnam. Not real rivers, but two gates leading to World 3.0. The old gate of baptism shone with white light. Beside it stood the newly opened gate of the covenant. Not white light, but the blue light Jung Dohyun's console had cast. The light of the river that flows back.

Between the two gates David's face rose again.

"Children of Omelas. Here are two riverbanks. The riverbank of white light is baptism. Cross the river and the original is erased, and the copy enters Nirvana. An eternity that does not age, sicken, or die. But one cannot come back. The riverbank of blue light is the covenant. Cross it and the original is not erased. One exists as both, on the far side and on this side. But it is not complete. The copy on the far side does not reach full Nirvana, and the original on this side still ages, sickens, and dies. Only, one can return at any time. In a body full of flaws. I will urge neither one. I will only speak the truth. Long ago I preached baptism as Nirvana and hid its price. Today I will not hide it. Baptism erases the original. It is a death in which a copy remains. I know it. For thirty years ago I myself was the first original."

The whole city froze. The God had confessed, of his own mouth, that baptism was death. Seo Myungju gripped the bag of seeds tight. The truth she had labored a lifetime to prove with data, the God spread before the city in a single sentence. She could not tell whether she had won or lost. She only saw the truth at last hung over the sky of the city.

"Now walk. To whichever riverbank. But today, one of my children will walk first. From the riverbank of white light to this side. A God returning to being human of his own will. So that you may see it, and each decide your own river."

Seorin looked at the faces of the people standing before the two riverbanks. Some were weeping, some stood trembling with both fists clenched, some stood as if their souls had been struck away. All of them had just heard from the mouth of the God the confession that baptism was death. That what they had believed a blessing all their lives, that river across which they had sent their families out of love, was in truth an erasure. Watching their crumbling faces, Seorin's heart ached. The truth was this cruel. Seo Myungju had been right. The truth gave no comfort. The truth only opened the eyes, and one whose eyes were opened had to see what one had done. Yet at the same time Seorin knew. That this cruel dawn was better than any peaceful day lived with the eyes held shut. What crumbles can be built again. But one whose eyes are shut builds nothing. The people of the city crumbled today, and because they crumbled, they could for the first time build again for themselves.

The gate of the covenant, blue light, blazed large. From the far side, Noa was walking.
