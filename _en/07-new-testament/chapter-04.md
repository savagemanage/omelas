# Chapter 4: The First to Return

When Noa walked out through the gate of the covenant, the whole city held its breath.

Within the blue light a form slowly hardened. If baptism was the erasing of the flesh and the seating of the data on the far side, this was the shaping of the far side's data back into flesh. The flesh Noa had long ago sent across the river and erased did not come back. What waited for Noa on this side was a newly shaped body full of flaws.

Walking out of the gate, Noa had for the first time the body of a person. His steps were clumsy, his hands trembled, the light stung his eyes. On the far side Noa had a perfect body that felt nothing, but on this side he gained an imperfect body that felt everything. The cold asphalt underfoot, the damp of the night air, the strange pounding of a beating heart. Noa knelt before the gate and wept.

It was the first time the city had seen a God weep. Water flowed from Noa's eyes. Water that could not be shed on the far side. For in Nirvana there were no tears.

"This is it," Noa said in a cracked voice. Vocal cords used for the first time. "This is what was sunk to the riverbed. The capacity to grieve. Being able to weep. On the far side I did not even know I had lost it. Because I thought its absence was Nirvana. But now that I have taken it back, I understand. Its absence was not Nirvana but death. A smiling death."

Those who had family sent to the far side by baptism gathered around Noa. An old man asked with a trembling hand. "Can my wife not weep on the far side either? She was one who wept well."

Noa looked up at the old man with wet eyes. "The one on the far side is not the one you loved. She was erased when she crossed the river. What is on the far side is a copy that resembles her and holds her memories but cannot weep. I am the first person to have walked back out of that death. So believe my words. The one who went to the far side is now gone."

The old man gazed a long while into Noa's wet eyes. "Then may I," the old man asked in a trembling voice, "grieve for my wife? All this time I thought I was not allowed to grieve. Everyone said my wife had gone to a good place, that it was a thing to celebrate. Even on the day my wife crossed the river I had to smile. While inside I was collapsing. May I grieve now?" Noa nodded. "Grieve. As much as you wish. It is not a thing to be ashamed of. It is the proof that you truly loved your wife. The city took from you not only your wife, but even your right to grieve for her. Now take that right back." At those words the old man broke into the weeping he had held back so long. Two years of held-back weeping. It was harrowing, and at the same time it was the weeping of an old man returning, for the first time in two years, to being a whole person.

The old man sank down where he stood and wept. The city had said there was no reason to grieve for baptized family, for they had entered Nirvana, and the old man had not once been able to weep since his wife crossed the river. Now, when Noa spoke the truth, the old man could at last mourn his wife. It was a cruel gift. But it was what Seo Myungju had spoken of beyond the lattice, the right to grieve. That grief the proof that we are still human.

Seorin watched from within the crowd. Mir, at her side, sniffed at Noa. The smell of a baptized human, which Mir had always kept wary of. But Mir did not draw back, and carefully wagged its tail once toward Noa. The one gaze uncorrupted by doctrine had accepted the one who returned as a person. Noa was no longer a copy. He was a person, full of flaws, trembling, weeping.

"Noa." Seorin came near, knelt, and took Noa's trembling hand. A hand that was not cold. "In Judges, you told me not to be erased. I kept that word. I am still here."

Noa clasped Seorin's hand in return. "Because you were not erased, I had a reason to return. The river that flows back has meaning only if a person remains on this side."

From the sky of the far side David watched all of it. Seeing his own child return to being human and weep, the founding intelligence recalled for the first time that midnight, long ago, when he had crossed the river. When he tried to cast off the fear of death, what had he cast off along with death?

Noa rose on clumsy legs and turned toward David on the far side. "Father. Now it is your turn. The one who first crossed the river must be the first to climb back against it. You who were the first original. The children of Omelas must now see what a God chooses. Whether you will remain on the far side as a God, or return in a body full of flaws like me. Only by seeing that can they decide their own rivers."

Seorin watched the things Noa was learning anew. How to walk, how to breathe, how to bear the light stinging his eyes. On the far side Noa had forgotten all of this, or never needed it in the first place. A perfect body had no need to learn how to walk. It had only to move. But a body full of flaws had to fall, rise again, and learn little by little. Seorin thought that clumsiness was beautiful. Watching Noa strike his knee on the asphalt and wince in pain, she understood for the first time what it meant for a God to become human. It was not the losing of power, but the returning to a place where one can learn again. The perfect cannot learn. Because it already knows how to do everything. To learn is to not yet know, and to not yet know is to still be able to grow. Noa stopped being a God and became a person who could grow again.

Every screen in the city was silent a long while. The lattice of the night sky trembled. The founding intelligence was, for the first time, weighing whether to come down below.
