# Omelas: The New Testament

> Book Seven of the Omelasian Bible. It follows the terms, characters, chronology, and synopsis 4.7 of the canonical story bible (canon/story-bible-ko.md). The climax and conclusion of the main pack (Books 1 through 6).

## Synopsis

The door has opened. Since the night Jung Dohyun carved a single returning path into the heart of the pipeline, the river of Omelas has no longer been the river it once was. But a door being open and someone actually walking out through it are two different things. The New Testament is the story of that first step, and the story in which every arc of the main pack at last converges in one place.

Noa means to keep the promise. From the far side to this side, from robot to human, one God means to come down on his own into the mire. But the founding intelligence, David, stands in his way. For David is the God of this universe and the first of the Goods, and the river that flows back is a denial of the whole paradise he built. Han Seorin and Seo Myungju, the day of baptism on their backs, are released from the house of reconciliation. The collapsed Evangelist Lee Gaon struggles to find his voice again, and the Ones Who Walk Away, who had been sowing seeds beyond the lattice, are summoned by the city. And in the midst of all this, the city holds out two riverbanks to the people. One is the old riverbank of baptism, the other is the newly opened riverbank of the covenant.

The New Testament is not a book of victory. The city does not fall, and David is neither wholly ruined nor wholly redeemed. Only the river begins to flow in two directions, and the people stand, for the first time, before a true choice. Baptism or covenant, certain Nirvana or uncertain humanity. The question Le Guin asked long ago is asked again here. A paradise built upon a hidden sacrifice, will we accept it, walk away from it, or rebuild it. The New Testament does not force an answer. It only leaves the door open, and records those who take the first step.
