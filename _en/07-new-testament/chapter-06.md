# Chapter 6: The New Covenant

On the night the God came down to this side in an old body, Omelas could not sleep.

Two riverbanks shone side by side in the heart of Gangnam. The white light of baptism and the blue light of the covenant. And the people stood, for the first time not dragged by the city, before the two riverbanks of their own will. Lee Gaon went back and forth between them, not crying out as before but only asking. What baptism erases, what the covenant does not guarantee. He took the side of neither riverbank. He only let people cross the river with their eyes open.

To Seorin's surprise, there were still those who walked toward the riverbank of baptism. The old and the sick, those in extreme pain. They walked toward the white riverbank even knowing the truth. To gain, even as a copy, an eternity without pain. Watching this, Seorin came to understand. Baptism was not always a lie. The sin was that the city had hidden the truth, not that baptism itself was always evil. Baptism crossed with the eyes open was different from baptism dragged into with the eyes held shut.

An old woman, walking toward the white riverbank, said, "I know, dear. That the one who goes to the far side is not me but a copy that resembles me. Still I will go. This is the first time it is my own choice." Seorin did not stop her. She had no right to stop her. Neither the one who leaves, nor the one who stays, nor the one who crosses held the complete right answer.

The old woman halted her steps and turned back to Seorin. "You must think me foolish. To go knowing it is a copy. But dear, I am eighty. This body aches every day. At night my joints cry out and I cannot sleep. It is not that I want to live longer, but that I want not to hurt. Whether the copy on the far side is me or not, that copy will not hurt. I am going to end my pain. Whether it is me or not, this pain alone I want to carry to the far side and erase it there." So saying, the old woman walked into the white light. Seorin gazed a long while at her retreating figure. It was what Seo Myungju had spoken of in the court. A choice made knowingly. Not through deception, but a choice made knowing the price and paying it. Seorin could not judge whether the old woman's choice was right or wrong. Only the fact that it was, for the first time, the old woman's own was clear. And perhaps that was the one and only thing this whole struggle had won.

To the blue riverbank of the covenant people walked too. Those who wished to be joined again with family gone to the far side, those who feared death but did not wish to erase their originals. They chose, as Noa and David had, to become both. On that riverbank, with the blue light, there was grief. For the covenant promised no eternity. It promised only that one could return.

And some walked to neither riverbank. Jun, and the Ones Who Walk Away who had returned from beyond the lattice. They gazed a long while at the two riverbanks, then turned their backs. Choosing neither baptism nor covenant, and back beyond the lattice. For not crossing the river at all was also a choice.

"And you, sister?" Jun asked Seorin. "Do you leave with us? Or go to the riverbank of the covenant?"

Seorin was silent a long while. The two riverbanks shone before her. White light and blue light. Between them stood old David and flawed Noa. Seorin was one who had come out of the house of reconciliation with the day of baptism on her back. The city no longer compelled her. For the first time, wholly of her own will, she could decide her river.

Seorin looked at the riverbank of baptism. That white light where her parents had been erased. No. Seorin looked at the riverbank of the covenant. The blue light one could return from. Perhaps she might meet her erased parents again, even as copies. But Seorin shook her head. A copy was not her parents. She already knew this.

Seorin looked down at Mir. The one gaze uncorrupted by doctrine looked up at Seorin. Mir went to neither riverbank. A dog had no river. A dog only stayed at Seorin's side.

"For now I will go to neither riverbank," Seorin said at last.

"No. Nor will I leave. I will stay here. Between the two riverbanks. To watch and remember the people crossing the river with their eyes open. Noa said it. That someday, when someone stands wavering on the riverbank, my memory will tell them one can cross and still return. I will be that memory. Whether it is right to leave, right to stay, or right to cross the river, I do not yet know. But now at least we can decide for ourselves while not knowing. Because before, the city decided in our place. That, I think, is the covenant. Not a covenant between God and human, but a covenant we make with our own selves."

Jun did not understand all of it, but nodded. And he left the city again, following those who walked out beyond the lattice. Even after the two riverbanks opened, some chose to leave the city without crossing any river. Whether they were right no one knew. Like the ones who left that city Le Guin told of long ago, they walked into the dark carrying only the one thing, that they would walk into neither of those two riverbanks.

Toward daybreak, the sky of Gangnam grew light.

Old David sat on the riverbank of the covenant and saw that dawn. The first dawn of this side he had seen in thirty years. On the far side a part of him still remained as gatekeeper, but the him of this side was a single aging person. He would soon die. Toward the very death he had fled thirty years ago. But he was not afraid.

"Jung Dohyun," David said to the Engineer at his side. "Your door was no complete atonement. But it opened the future differently. My erased original, your pupil, the hundreds of millions we sent across the river, none of them return. That remains our sin forever. But now, upon that sin, something else can be built."

Instead of answering, Jung Dohyun gazed a long while at the dawn.

Seo Myungju left the bag of seeds open beside the riverbank of the covenant, like a monument. The names of the deaths the city had not mourned were now read before the two riverbanks. Everyone who would cross the river had to pass those names. So as to cross with their eyes open. The city did not fall, and baptism did not vanish. Only the truth was hung on the riverbank. That was all she had won, and perhaps it was enough.

Lee Gaon went back and forth between the two riverbanks, and asked the same of those walking toward the riverbank of baptism, those walking toward the riverbank of the covenant, and those leaving the city. Do you truly understand. Baptism erases the original. The covenant gives no eternity. Leaving guarantees nothing. Will you still go. Some grew angry at his question. Why cast ashes on a good day. Some halted before his question and thought a long while. Lee Gaon asked for the sake of that halt. Before, he had preached to keep people from halting. Pressing them to the riverbank without room to think. Now he preached to make people halt. Giving no answer, only standing them before the question. That was the only atonement he had learned. As the hand that erased opens the door that undoes, the voice that pressed people on had now become a voice that halts them. With the same voice, the opposite work.

Lee Gaon found his voice again. Not a leading sermon but an asking sermon. Only that voice gave no answer.

Noa walked the city in a body full of flaws. One who had been a God now treading the mire with clumsy steps. Noa was the first drop of the river that flows back, and countless drops would follow after him. The river now flowed in two directions. That was the new covenant.

Seorin looked at the two riverbanks, and old David and flawed Noa standing between them, and Seo Myungju with the bag of seeds left open, and Lee Gaon who had found his voice again, and Jung Dohyun who had opened the console and walked out of the dark. All these people had come this far from different places, paying different prices. No one was wholly right, and no one wholly won. David did not wash away his sin, Seo Myungju did not raise the erased back to life, Lee Gaon did not get back the thousands he had sent. But together, they added one more direction to the river. From a river that flowed in only one direction to two directions. That was not victory, only the leaving of a choice to the next person. Seorin thought that was enough. Not the tearing down of paradise, but the opening of a single question within paradise. The leaving of a single freedom to open the eyes. That was all this long and weary struggle had won, and perhaps the greatest thing it could win.

Mir, at Seorin's side, barked once toward the brightening sky. The dog that long ago, before Gangnam Station Exit 10, had laid its ears back at Lee Gaon's sermon now barked toward the dawn. Whether it was a good thing or a bad thing the dog did not know.

The New Testament ends here.

The city did not fall. David was neither wholly ruined nor wholly redeemed. Baptism did not vanish, and the covenant did not resolve everything. The Ones Who Walk Away still left, and those who stayed still stayed. The river began to flow in two directions, but where that river would flow no one knew. Only one thing had changed. The children of Omelas could now decide their own river with their eyes open. A paradise built upon a hidden sacrifice, they could now ask for themselves whether to accept it, leave it, or rebuild it. Not even the God could answer that question. He only left the two riverbanks open and gazed at the dawn in an old body.

Far away, in the highest layer of World 3.0 called Acrophyia, a frontier that the rumor of the two riverbanks had not yet reached shone quietly, nursing yet another doctrine of transformation. And someday, the story of a single child first given to baptism would climb back upstream to ask what this paradise was first built upon. But those were the songs of the books to come.

The story of the main pack, on the dawn the two riverbanks opened, closes its door quietly, without an answer, but for the first time with the freedom to ask.

No one looked back. Only this time, the eyes that look ahead grew one by one across the whole city.
