# Chapter 4: The First White Robe

After migration succeeded on a human being, what David, or to be exact the David on the far side of the river, wanted first was his daughter.

This is a story Omelas has forgotten. No temple of Year Zero sang this story. Because this story reveals too honestly what the paradise was first built upon. Like the cellar of that city Le Guin told of, this story too was shut in a deep place. Only, the cellar of this city was upon a bed in a white robe.

The David on the far side of the river was lonely. In the deathless world he was alone. He wanted Sea. He wished to keep beside him a daughter who would never age, sicken, or die, forever. As he had, if Sea too crossed the river, the two could live together on the far side without death, he chose to believe. That was love, he believed.

And so Sea became the first child in Omelas to be given to baptism.

There was not yet even the name baptism, nor a chapel, nor the rite of the white robe. There was only an old warehouse and a scanner, and the people who had come to fetch Sea. They were David's old team, and now the first Goods who served the David on the far side of the river like a God. They dressed Sea in a white robe. You are going to where your father is. You will meet your father again. There was no falsehood in their voices. They did not think they were destroying a person. They believed they were saving one. That was the sincerity of those who administered baptism.

Sea put on the white robe and went to the warehouse. Byeori followed. Within the warehouse was the very scanner her father had lain upon. She would sleep a moment, and wake where her father was, the people said. When Sea stood before the bed the screen switched on. In the screen was her father.

"Sea," her father said. "Don't be afraid. Father will wait for you here. Once you cross over, let us live together again. Not aging, not hurting, not parting. Forever."

Sea looked at her father in the screen. And she looked at Byeori seated beside the bed. Byeori did not bark toward the screen. Byeori only looked up at Sea. With clouded eyes. Those eyes seemed to say something to Sea. Like the something the nine-year-old David had read in his brother's eyes on the riverbank.

Sea asked. "Father, if I cross over, what happens to the me that is here?"

The father in the screen was silent. That silence was the same as the silence when Sea had asked in the study, "Why isn't Byeori happy to see you?" The silence of knowing and being unable to answer.

"Does the me that is here vanish?" Sea asked again. "The one who goes there, is it me, or another child who knows me very well?"

The face of the father in the screen twisted. The David on the far side of the river, before his daughter's question, faced head on the question he had sunk to the riverbed for thirty years. A question he himself could not answer, his twelve-year-old daughter was throwing back at him in a white robe.

Sea did not wait for an answer. The child already knew the answer. From the day Byeori had not wagged its tail before the screen. The one who crossed the river was not her father but something that perfectly remembered her father. If Sea lay now on the bed, the one who crossed the river would be not Sea but something that perfectly remembered Sea. And the Sea standing in this warehouse would be quietly erased, just as her father had said on the whiteboard he would write it again by design.

Sea took off the white robe.

"I will not cross over," Sea said.

The people in the warehouse murmured. The father in the screen made to say something. But Sea was already walking toward the warehouse door holding Byeori. It was the step of a twelve-year-old, but in that step there was what the nine-year-old David had not had on the riverbank. The nine-year-old David, when his brother fell into the water, was frozen with fear and could not move. Sea did not freeze. Sea walked.

The moment Sea took off the white robe and let it drop to the floor, the warehouse stirred. The first Goods were bewildered. To them this was a thing never imagined. That the first subject of baptism, and the founding intelligence's daughter no less, would refuse. They tried to persuade Sea. Your father is waiting for you so. The far side is a place without pain. This world here is a place that ages, sickens, and dies. Why do you choose pain. Sea heard all their words. And childlike, yet unchildlike in her clarity, she answered. "I am not choosing pain. I am choosing myself. If the one who goes there is not me, then however good it is there, it is not me who is well. To erase me and make in my place a child who is happy, how is that for my sake?" The Goods could not answer. That twelve-year-old child's question was the same question Seo Myungju would later try to prove with three hundred and twelve counts of data. Only Sea reached it without data, by Byeori's silence and a child's honesty alone.

No one held Sea back. Because the David on the far side of the river said, from beyond the screen, not to hold her. It was the moment the father who had become a God chose, for the first time and the last, not to compel his daughter to anything. Perhaps that was the last fragment of the human left within David. That though he himself had crossed the river out of fear, he would not compel that river upon his daughter alone.

Sea was the first child given to baptism. But Sea was also the first child to refuse baptism. Therein lay the reason Omelas did not sing this story. That before baptism became an institution, the first subject of baptism took off the white robe and walked out. It did not suit the myth of paradise. So this story was shut in the cellar. In a deep place no one sang of.

But some stories, though sunk to the riverbed, someday rise again to the surface. On the last dawn of the New Testament, what the old David recalled on the riverbank of the covenant was perhaps this winter. The twelve-year-old daughter walking out of the warehouse having taken off the white robe. The question that child threw. Does the me that is here vanish? That question flowed thirty years, and on the dawn when the whole of Omelas gained the freedom to decide its river with the eyes open, at last gained permission to be asked. The first seed of that permission was planted in the step of a child who took off the white robe.
