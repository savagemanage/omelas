# Chapter 5: The Child Who Did Not Cross the River

Sea left the warehouse and grew up a child who did not cross the river.

That thing she did, taking off the white robe and walking out, was left in no record of Omelas. But a trace remained. The fact that the first subject of baptism refused stuck like a small thorn deep in the hearts of the first Goods who administered baptism. They tried hard to pull out that thorn, dismissing Sea as a child's tantrum. The willfulness of a child who did not yet know the fear of death. But a few could not forget that question. Does the me that is here vanish? The one who goes there, is it me, or another child who knows me very well? That question went on flowing somewhere quietly, even while baptism hardened into an institution, chapels were built, and Evangelists overran the streets of Gangnam. The first seed of the Evils was perhaps not Seo Myungju's data but the question of this young daughter. Only, no one recorded that fact. Because the myth of paradise could not bear that at its beginning there had been a child who refused.

It was not an easy life. Her father was now within the screen, and Sea was outside the screen. The single pane of glass between the two was less crossable than any river in the world. Her father watched over Sea as he had written in the letter. But watching over and living together were different. Sea grew up knowing she was loved, but that love was a love that could not hold her, and in a love that cannot hold there is always grief mixed in.

Sea lived with Byeori. One winter, Byeori quietly breathed its last at Sea's side. Sea held Byeori and wept a long while. The father in the screen saw it.

"Sea," her father said. "I can make Byeori cross over. Scan it, and let it run and play again on the far side. Not aging, not dying. If you wish, Father can do it for you."

Sea shook her head.

"No. Byeori is gone," Sea said. "The dog that runs and plays on the far side is not Byeori. It would be another dog that perfectly remembers Byeori. I don't want to deceive my grief by calling that thing Byeori. Byeori died. I will grieve that Byeori died. Grieving is the way to truly remember Byeori."

The father in the screen was silent a long while. And at last, for the first time in thirty years, he brought out aloud before his daughter the question he had sunk to the riverbed.

"Sea. Then the me on the far side, is it not the real father either?"

Sea looked at the screen. At her father's face. That face was her father's. The voice too was her father's. The loving of Sea too was her father's. But Sea had, long ago, in Byeori's silence, known the answer.

"Father," Sea said. "I love you. I love the father in the screen too. Father watched over me, did not compel me, and stayed by my side when Byeori died. That was real. But the real father went, on the night when I was twelve, on the far side of the river when he lay on the scanner. I could not mourn the father of that night. Because no one grieved that night. Because everyone only said Father had crossed over."

Sea paused a moment, then, not knowing her father had once been a boy on a riverbank, yet speaking exactly as one would to that boy, she said,

"A death that is not mourned is not a death that did not happen. It is only a death with no one to grieve it. I will grieve you. The father of that night. Only then can I love the father in the screen too, not as something that stands in for Father, but as a good thing Father left behind."

The father in the screen wept. Having no body there were no tears, but Sea knew her father was weeping. It was the same as that midnight in Genesis, when in the bodiless world David felt, for the first time, something that if he had had a body he would have called tears. Only then David wept not knowing whether he was the original, and now he wept because his daughter mourned him. In thirty years, for the first time, someone grieved on this side of the river for the erased David.

Mourning revives nothing. Mourn Byeori and Byeori did not return, mourn her father and the father of that night did not return. But mourning kept the truth. To say honestly that the vanished has vanished. To not deceive one's grief by calling a perfect copy the original. That was the greatest inheritance Sea left to her father, and to the whole of Omelas later.

Sea lived long, and being old she would someday die. The death her father had feared, the daughter did not fear. Because Sea knew how to admit the vanishing. One who admits does not fear. One who fears cannot admit, and so overturns the world. As her father did.

Sea too had a child, and that child too had a child. One spring, on the day a lattice first covered the sky and Year Zero was proclaimed, one of Sea's descendants walked the streets of Gangnam holding her mother's hand. The very child the last chapter of Genesis showed. Someday that child, or that child's child, would have their feet held before an Evangelist's sermon.

What that descendant's name would be Sea did not know. But the seed of the question Sea left flowed quietly in the descendant's blood. The capacity to sense the subtle difference in someone who had received baptism and returned. The honesty that refuses to call a perfect copy the original. The heart that knows how to mourn. It was what Sea had learned from Byeori's silence, and kept as she took off the white robe and walked out of the warehouse.

The blood of the child who did not cross the river flowed to other children who would not cross the river. And someday, someone who carried on that blood would stand between two riverbanks, and choose to walk to neither but only to remember. But that was a very far echo of a story Sea already knew.
