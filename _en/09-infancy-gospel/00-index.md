# Omelas: Infancy Gospel

## Synopsis

It turns time back to before even Genesis, and into the seams of Genesis. This book lays two childhoods over each other. One is the young David of a certain winter in the Old Calendar, before he first feared death. The other is his daughter Sea, the first child in Omelas to be laid upon the bed of baptism after her father crossed the river.

Genesis wrote that David felt death at forty-nine. But the fear was not born that night. From a certain far younger winter, from the day a boy lost something on a riverbank, it had been growing within him. The Infancy Gospel climbs back to that first winter and asks again, at a child's eye level, what the paradise called Omelas was first built upon.

And from the place the father erased, the daughter walks out. Sea grows up with a single letter her father left and one old dog. Her father said he would come back in another form. Sea comes to face, in a twelve-year-old body, what that promise means. Like the child shut in the cellar of Le Guin's Omelas, in the first winter of this paradise too there was a child, not in a cellar but upon a bed in a white robe.
