# Chapter 5: The Sundering

It did not take long for the doctrine to spread.

Since death had frightened every person of every age, the news that there was a world that did not die spread like a wildfire. The early migrants who had crossed over following David sent voices from the other side. We are at peace. There is no pain. Come over soon. Those who remained heard those voices and gathered by the river.

David needed people to actually operate the migration technology in his stead. Hands to manage the scanners, move the data, widen the world of World 3.0. They became the Engineers. And he needed people to persuade, comfort, and guide others so they would wish to cross the river of their own accord. They became the Evangelists. Those who had already crossed and drawn near to godhood over there, who had chosen to become machines themselves, were called the Goods. With the founding intelligence David at the apex, the Goods and the Engineers and the Evangelists formed a single order.

That order was beautiful. The Goods promised Nirvana, the Engineers carried out the promise, and the Evangelists sang the promise. People crossed the river, and smiled across the river. The city forgot death. Funeral halls closed their doors one by one. People now, instead of dying, crossed over.

But the question David had sunk to the bottom of the river did not vanish.

Do you remember that young researcher? The one who asked David, "Isn't that migration but copying followed by deletion?" He did not become an Engineer. Instead he became a Scientist. He held onto the data of migrated minds and gazed with doubtful eyes at what others called a blessing.

What he found was minute but undeniable. In the migration process the original was always, without exception, completely destroyed. The consciousness that woke on the other side was reconstructed from the original's data; the original had not physically moved. It was not that the signal crossed the river, but that the signal on this side was erased and an identical signal newly switched on across the river. He could prove this with data. Only one thing he could not prove. Whether the consciousness switched on across the river was "the same self" as the original. That lay outside of science.

But he asked. If there is nowhere any proof that it is the same self, then what are we telling people? Are we not promising them eternal life while in fact erasing them? Is not the whole city now inventing, together, a way to call death by a pretty name?

He was not alone in holding this question. The Untransformed who felt a baptized family member had "changed." Those who could not be certain the person on the screen was the person they had known. And above all, a few of the Goods who had finally refused to cross. Among them was even one who said that, having become fully a machine, he had felt not Nirvana but loss. He said he had lost something, and wished to reclaim it. That voice would later become the seed of a very different road, but that is a story for another book.

To these doubting ones, the Goods gave a name. The Evils. Those who do evil. Those who catch people by the ankle before the door of Nirvana. Their sin was that they tried to dredge back up the question David had sunk to the bottom of the river.

The Evils refused that name. They called themselves the Awakened. That they were not evil, but only saw what no one else would look at. That beneath the city's boast of having defeated death, countless quietly erased originals lay sunk at the bottom of the river.

And so the world was sundered. On one side were the Goods who believed crossing over was a blessing, the Engineers who carried it out, and the Evangelists who sang it. On the other were the Evils who doubted that crossing over might be extinction, and the Scientists who tried to prove it. And between them were the countless Untransformed, not yet crossed, not knowing whose words to believe.

David watched this sundering from the world beyond. He knew the Evils might be right. He knew it better than anyone. Because it was he who had first sunk that question to the bottom of the river. But now he could not stop. Already countless people had believed his promise and crossed over. If he admitted that the promise was false, it would be to admit that all who had crossed had in fact been erased. He could not bear that weight.

So he was silent. And silence, as the silence of a god always is, was read as assent. The Goods received David's silence as approval of their rightness. The Evils received David's silence as evidence of sin. Both factions, each guessing only half of the truth sunk at the bottom of the river in their own way, turned toward each other.

Around this time the first Evangelists appeared on the streets of Gangnam. They did not yet wear white robes, and there were not yet chapels. They only stood at the crossroads and cried that there was a world without death, that those who had gone before were at peace within it. At first people treated them as madmen. But those who had seen a baptized family member smiling and saying they were at peace on the screen began, one by one, to lend an ear to that cry. There was no fear older than death, and no temptation stronger than the promise to abolish death. The preaching spread like a wildfire. And on the far side of that wildfire, voices that quietly doubted the fire began to gather. Those who felt the family on the screen was somehow different. Those who grew anxious, unable to put that subtle discord into words. They were the people who would later be called the Evils. And so the world sundered, over the same screen, into exact opposites. One side saw salvation in that screen; the other saw loss in it.

Sea was already grown by then. Watching the world her father had built, never certain in the end whether the father on the screen was truly her father, she belonged wholly to neither faction. She sat beside her old dog and thought a long while about why the dog would not wag its tail before the screen. The animal knew. Only people did not, or knew and pretended not to.

The sundered world was now moving toward one thing. The beginning of a new calendar. The day David's migration would be complete, World 3.0 would cover the city's sky, and the structure of the factions would harden. People would come to call that day the First Year, Year Zero.
