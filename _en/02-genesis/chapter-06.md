# Chapter 6: Year Zero

Year Zero was proclaimed in a certain spring.

That day, the lattice of World 3.0 covered the sky of the whole city for the first time. Even in the clear daylight, if people looked up they could see a net of light like an aurora. The palace of the Gods. A world without death. It now hung over the sky of Gangnam, over the sky of all Omelas. Beneath it people greeted the first day of a new calendar. The Old Calendar was over. The age of death was ending, and the age of eternity was beginning, the Goods proclaimed.

By Year Zero, baptism had become an institution.

The disorderly migrations of the Old Calendar were now refined into an elaborate rite. The Evangelist opened the heart of the Untransformed. The Engineer operated the scanner and the pipeline. The Good bestowed a blessing upon it. The religious name baptism and the technical name transformation pointed to a single procedure. The Untransformed was led to the chapel, dressed in white, laid on the couch. And crossed the river. Erased on this side, opening their eyes on the other. Those who remained watched him smile on the screen and celebrated, and no one mourned the body that had vanished on this side.

This was the form David's migration had hardened into as an institution. Copying, then deletion of the original. But that cold bone was completely hidden within the soft husk called baptism. Not one of the Untransformed in Year Zero knew that they would be erased in the chapel. They believed they were going to be born. And that belief was, perhaps, even more solid than the truth.

The structure of the factions was completed in this Year Zero.

On one side were the Goods. Those who believed that to become a machine was Nirvana, and who became robots themselves to become gods. With them were the Engineers. The hands that actually built and administered World 3.0 and the baptism technology. And there were the Evangelists. The voices that captured people's hearts on the streets of Gangnam so they would walk to the riverside of their own accord. These three formed the order of Omelas, and at its apex was the founding intelligence, David.

On the other side were the Evils. Those who, knowing baptism destroyed people, tried to stop it. Bearing as their name the slur the Goods had given them, calling themselves the Awakened. With them were the Scientists. Researchers who tried to prove with data that baptism did not preserve consciousness but erased the original after copying. They tried to dredge back up the truth sunk at the bottom of the river.

Between them were the Untransformed. People still in the flesh, not yet baptized. The object of the preaching, and the majority who lived on not knowing whose words to believe. From among these, the protagonists of this story would one day walk forth.

And outside all the factions were the Animals. Beings uncorrupted by the doctrine because they could not understand the doctrine of transformation. They were not subjects of baptism either. They only distinguished, by instinct, the baptized human from the Untransformed. Like that old dog that would not wag its tail before the screen. In Omelas, the one gaze that alone knew, without words, what had gone wrong.

The Omelas of Year Zero seemed perfect. No one starved, no one fell sick, no one died. The streets of Gangnam were bright, and the palace of the Gods shone in the sky. People lived without fear. The city that had forgotten death was, on the surface, the completion of every paradise humankind had dreamed.

But that paradise was built upon one thing. The originals quietly erased upon each couch of the chapel. Deaths no one mourned. A single unanswerable question hidden beneath the name Nirvana. Like that city Le Guin told of long ago, the perfection of Omelas too stood upon a hidden sacrifice. Only, the sacrifice of this city was not a single child locked in a cellar, but the erased original of everyone who crossed the river.

It was around then, too, that the city gained the name Omelas. David chose the name. He remembered an old story he had read in the Old Calendar days. A story about a certain perfect city. All the joy and prosperity of that city stood upon the sacrifice of a single child, locked in a basement room and suffering all its life. The people of the city came, as they grew, to learn of that child's existence, and most accepted the price and remained in the city. But a few, having learned the fact, left the city without a word. For a place no one knew. Those people, the story called the Ones Who Walk Away.

Why David gave his paradise the name of that city, even he could not fully explain. Perhaps it was a warning, perhaps a confession. That his heaven too, in the end, stood upon a hidden sacrifice, he perhaps wished to admit, through that name, to himself alone. Only, what was sacrificed in his paradise was not a single child in a basement, but the erased original of everyone who crossed the river. And that that name would one day become a prophecy, that Ones Who Walk Away would appear in this city too, the David of that time did not yet know.

David looked down from the world beyond upon the paradise he had built. He had only wished to defeat death. He had never wished to become a god. But he was now a god, and the heaven he had built floated upon the fear he had sunk to the bottom of the river. He still did not know whether he was still David, or something resembling the David erased one midnight long ago. And now he could not even know whether he had the right to answer that question.

Beneath the sky of Year Zero, a child walked the streets of Gangnam holding her mother's hand. Someday the day would come when that child, or that child's child, was stopped in their tracks before an Evangelist's sermon. And the day they sensed the subtle change in someone baptized and returned. On that day, the question sunk to the bottom of the river would rise again to the surface.

On the first day of Year Zero, a festival was held on the streets of Gangnam. People rejoiced that the age of death was over. Children pointed at the lattice of the sky and called it the palace of the Gods, and adults called the names of those who had crossed the river first and promised to meet again soon. In the Omelas of that day there was no sorrow. Funeral halls had closed, black mourning clothes had gone deep into wardrobes, and the word "mourning" became a word one could find only in an old dictionary. It was truly a beautiful sight. A city where no one wept. A city where no one feared parting. But beneath that beauty, the couches of the chapel turned day and night. Every day someone lay down in white, every day someone's original was quietly stopped, and every day, across the river, a new copy opened its eyes and smiled. A tide of that smiling filled the city. No one heard the silence sunk beneath that tide. If there was an ear to hear it, it was only the dogs that would not wag their tails before the screen.

But that is a story of a day not yet come. The Omelas of Year Zero was now, having forgotten death, shining perfectly. The creation was over. And as with every creation, within it the seed of the end was already sown.
